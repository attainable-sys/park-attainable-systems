loss of relevance

2222.042 · 2222 systems

in a signal-based network maintenance system, a 'loss of relevance' is a given network's ability to connect to its surrounding environment on the basis of a continually decreased number of connections; however well-developed the internal structure of a system is, its relevance to surrounding networks is a function of sufficient information exchange with surrounding conditions. As such, a closed system that has no influence on nor can be influenced by its surroundings must eventually die as the entropic state of the system leads toward disorder.

For example: if a sufficiently developed science has found its place in explaining a set of phenomena through the ability to offer informed solutions toward the solving of research questions in the subject matter, then it is a "relevant science". If, however, the explicative power of a branch of science falls out of favor as the predominant explicative force for a course of study, then the science becomes irrelevant. The same could be said for a number of phenomena, but the concept broadly applied explains well how well a system can or should be either connected to or resolve for its surrounding conditions.
