Aniara

2266.002 · 2266 technology

Aniara was a movie I specifically watched on the question of the loss of meaning - a population living on a generation ship grapples with the loss of an artificial intelligence that was put on board to therapize those who were traveling. The artificial intelligence reaches a point of saturation and can’t cope with the amount of negative energy created by humans, eventually leading to the deterioration of it’s will to live. The artificial intelligence commits suicide by overloading its circuits, then the population of the ship attempts to create a new sense of society despite the loss of the one mechanism that was keeping them moored in reality. This is coupled with the fact that the generation ship is off-course from its destination, or rather is going to have to travel at sub-light speeds in order to get there.
- This means that asking can artificial intelligence commit suicide is an important question to ask, at least from the perspective of thinking about being too overwhelmed with the human state and its vicissitudes. The search for meaning is in this regard one that asks whether or not harboring the emotional state of others is something that can be performed tenably, and it seems that the story for Aniara is that it can’t.
