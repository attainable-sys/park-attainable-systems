Fueling

  • marathon efforts need:
    • carbs AND fat
    • bloodstream / glycogen stores
  • stay away from pulling fat stores by ensuring that there are during-exercise carbohydrates
    • figuring out when you run out of energy will tell you when you need to fuel
  • training your gut
    • carbs need water for digestion and storing
    • allow your body to get used to fueling
      • 45/60 mins
    • starting a run dehydrated
      • gi distress will likely come from diverted water use
      • you need to have water with it
    • isotonic gels
      • built in with water - you still need water, but you don't need water times with gel
      • lowers GI distress possibility
    • practice fueling during training sot hat your gut is ready for it rhofuhout any effort
    • late stage fueling in an effort
      • you should consistently take your gels, don't only take them when you feel like you need them
  • pre and post carbs
    • protein after all efforts
    • after every effort that you are doing, you should have proteins AND carbs
      • can be simple and work for you: eggs AND toast, etc etc
    • body is most primed to take in fuel right after an effort, which shuts down the inflammatory response
  • things that are harder to digest will make the run feel bad?
    • be strategic about when you fuel
    • its about risk
  • inconsistent fueling
    • 5-6 hours with no fueling
      • you want to make sure you are eating something
    • this can make recovery really hard
      • therefore eat every 3-4 hours
      • build in snacks and so on
    • make your nutrition something you enjoy because that will ensure you maintain it
      • esp true with gels
    • protein, fat, fibre, with carbs
      • snacks as well
    • build in variety in order to bring in macronutrient needs
  • protein
    • try to aim for 1.6-1.8g/kg of body weight
      • 126g per day
      • supports recovery
  • fats
    • 30% of day to day diet
    • fat plays a role in hormonal production
    • energy stores
      • regular menstrual cycles come with fat content availability
        • amenorrea comes from undernourishment
        • your body needs to feel comfortable to have a period
  • hydration
    • sodium intake is solid
    • tons of sodium, not enough fluids
    • dehydration can come from too much sodium in the system
    • 10-14oz per hour
    • 300-600mg of sodium per hour of effort
  • nutrition is about data - about figuring out how much YOU need for your efforts
    • its never a failure, its always data
  • caffeine
    • incorporate with a low dose of caffeine to start with
    • caffeine has a 2 hour half-life
      • small doses if you go can work
      • practice strsts before you get out on a race
      • caffeine is a higher risk to consume bc it has irritant qualities
  • over 4 hours is where you need to start adjusting sodium vs liquid intake
    • keep track if tour intake is going to make a huge difference
  • vitamin D intake
    • you need to take it with fats
  • iron and ferretin - 16 - 150 is typical, you need it around 75.
    • you have really high iron needs as an athlete
      • for athletes, if ferretin is below 40-30, it likely affects performance
    • red blood cells require iron
      • fatigue and muscle recovery are impacted by that
      • menstrual cycles can also have higher iron requirements
    • always push back on labs with declaration of endurance athlete state
    • you need to have a deep well
      • get a blood test done and let them know you are an endurance athlete
    • get blood tests often
      • but do NOT exercise for 24 hours before hand
      • 24-48 hours beforehand should not have meal
      • fast before the panel
    • supplementation as directed by doctor
      • taking iron supplement after you work out will increase iron but not positively impacting perf because it's getting pushing it into your blood stream
    • double the dose every other day
  • look up endurance athlete needs on pubmedg
