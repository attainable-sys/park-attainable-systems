Private Library, 29 August 2023: privacy as the foundation of trust

1166.001 · 1166 heliax / namada · 2023.08.29

Discussing Taking Trust Seriously in Privacy Law - Hartzog et al,

The 230829 Private Library call concerned itself the most with how to pull ourselves away from a cultural paradigm of privacy pessimism toward one of privacy optimism by engaging in Privacy as foundational to Trust with others, thus as the basis for building relationships and community.

At the highest level - well-founded communities built on trust and the relationships that they share form the basis of the most resilient communities. Signaling more widely is a function of the information we have chosen for others to know, but there is no reason to broadcast widely that which we are not ready to share with others.

What is privacy pessimism?

privacy pessimism is the framing of individualistic privacy being that which protects the individual from the horrors of a world that seeks to impose itself upon us - further decrying a 'death of privacy' in a time where the world has crept in to consume all of our individuality.
- Pessimism is a tool that can be used by intermediaries to navigate control over your privacy away from the tools that you use and toward the control illusion - wherein you as the individual are fully responsible for your own privacy.
- Within the scope of our interactions with other tech, we 'give up' our privacy in order to have certain benefits - which means that we 'pay' for free things not just with that which should be private to us and while having our right to choose whether or not we want to engage in this trust taken from us.
- the Control Illusion makes it such that we have to do things like read entire privacy policies in order to be 'responsible' for our privacy
- It was pointed out by a community member that "education" is necessary for individuals to appreciate their responsibility to their own privacy
- This was responded to by asking if this unduly places a burden on the individual to engage in this education, rather than navigating toward building better relationships between those things we are forced to trust and the individuals that use these things.
- In previous discussions, we highlighted that 'total privacy' is isolating - meaning that there must be some function within which privacy must act as a shield toward engagement with others.
- when we build relationships with others, we engage in building trust, because trust is a function of the information that is shared between parties - when we choose not to build trust, we do not have relationships with others.
- This is true of technology as well - when we spoke to how auditors earn their reputations, the incumbency of a first mover advantage was brought up - but with the understanding that new entrants can win out in the long term provided they build a relationship, through trust, with those they audit - and this is an optimistic approach.
- In the use of most modern internet technology, we are coerced into trusting what they do with our information, and truly do not know what they do with it.
- This asks the question of whether or not we should establish a new paradigm of accountability and data stewardship among those that should be held accountable for their use of our information - and we should!
- Not mentioned: part of the article concerned itself with the pathos of Privacy as a sacrifice - that our experience on the internet is one of giving up our privacy in order to have 'a good experience' on the internet, meaning that privacy somehow is argued as not being a positive user experience.

What is Privacy Optimism?

privacy optimism, by contrast, engages in the ideal that we can disclosure of private information (or a crucial volume) is foundational to trust, because the more information that someone holds about you, the more vulnerable you are as an individual.
* The implications of this are that all interactions between individuals and the technologies and other parties with whom they interact are build on the basis of loyalty and trust.
* While we work to build trust-minimized technology, we seek to build a foundation for decreasing vulnerability in the users that participate in its use.
* This requires accountability on the part of the parties with whom we interact and build relationships, whether people or otherwise.
* A culture of privacy optimism requires equal parts transparency and autonomy built on trust, so (as mentioned by StellarMagnet) - the relationships that you build with your community should be intentional.
* What if who you truly are is distasteful to those that you interact with? - asked by dismad
* Answering this, the question of your 'reach' is not to be called into view, because a community is not built on trying to get as many people as possible to listen to you, but rather those with whom you develop a network of relationships based on trust.
* Workin from a position of privacy optimism means that working on Namada is a function of those who choose to continue working on it and the relationships they have built in order to do so.
