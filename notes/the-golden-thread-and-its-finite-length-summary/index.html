The Golden Thread and its Finite Length (summary)

1050.052 · 1050 writing

Theme: There is only so far that you can go with the knowledge that you hold and the ways that it can get you into the world - once you let go of the thread, there's nothing saying that you'll actually make your way out of the labyrinth.
This is the central premise - the thread is of a finite length and you cannot make it longer - imagine if Theseus had made it into the labyrinth with the golden thread offered by Ariadne and the thread only allowed him to go so far into the labyrinth, and further, that it was letting go of the thread that made the Minotaur into a beast. Ariadne's madness is an obsession with that which can never be fully known, the boundaries of the labyrinth existing within its folds, greatly hoping to sacrifice into the Night the hubris of this man who seeks to
The thematic moral of this story is that once you let go of the thread, you might not find your way back, and this is something for which you'll have to deal with the consequences.

Summary:

1. The Labyrinth as Symbol:
The labyrinth is a symbol of connection to one's old self and the outside world, representing a plunge into self-altering depths. It also represents Ariadne's inner fears and desires.

2. Cosmic Horror Elements:
The story incorporates Lovecraftian themes of incomprehensible realities and the insignificance of humanity in the broader cosmos. This is reflected in the mysterious and eerie nature of the labyrinth and the Minotaur.

3. Exploration of Human Psychology:
The narrative explores human psychology, particularly Ariadne's inner struggles and fears, as well as Theseus' desire to conquer her and the unknown. The story delves into the themes of self-discovery, love, and the consequences of seeking too much knowledge.

"I give to you of me that which I believe I should be made of"

  • Ariadne's madness is her love of knowledge and its boundaries
  • A question of what it means for the virgins to be sacrificed into the labyrinth, then who is theseus? What does he mean to the labyrinth, and why is he any different from the virgins who are sacrificed?
  • What if it was a slot canyon instead of a labyrinth?
  • An option:
    • He makes it back out of the labyrinth, but he has lost himself, he is barely himself, he leaves her behind, knowing too much that there is too much for him to know, his hubris defeated.

Structure

  1. Setting and Background

    • Athens and Crete: Describe the socio-political climate and the historic burden borne by Athens due to past transgressions against Crete.

    • The Labyrinth: Highlight its construction by Daedalus, its symbolic significance as a structure that’s both a physical and psychological labyrinth.
      2. Character Profiles

    • Theseus: Present him as a blend of hero and seeker, driven by both a sense of duty and a deep, intrinsic pull towards the Labyrinth.

    • Ariadne: Detail her complex relationship with the Labyrinth, oscillating between reverence and fear, and her tragic role as both victim and manipulator.

    • Daedalus: Though more of a background character, mention his role as the architect, possibly hinting at his own reservations or deeper knowledge about the labyrinth.
      3. Key Themes to Explore

    • The Search for Meaning: How Theseus’s quest in the Labyrinth parallels the existential search for meaning in life.

    • Transformation and Identity: Explore how Theseus’s journey into the labyrinth mirrors his internal transformation, leading him to lose his identity and eventually become part of the myth.

    • Reality vs. Illusion: The Labyrinth as a metaphor for life’s complexities and the illusions we chase, believing them to be truths.
      4. Plot Points

    • Introduction and Journey to Crete: Set the stage with Theseus’s arrival in Crete and his initial encounter with Ariadne.

    • The Gift of the Thread: Ariadne giving Theseus the golden thread, charged with symbolic weight, acting as his guide and eventual tether to sanity.
    • Descent into the Labyrinth: Detailed descriptions of his journey deeper into the labyrinth, emphasizing the changing environment and his psychological state.
    • Abandonment of the Thread: A pivotal moment where Theseus, reaching the end of the thread, chooses to delve deeper into the unknown, symbolizing the abandonment of his last connection to reality.

    • Transformation into the Minotaur/Black Dog: Theseus’s realization that there is no Minotaur other than himself, culminating in his complete transformation and loss of self.
      5. Climax and Conclusion

    • Theseus’s Realization: His slow-burning realization of Ariadne’s true intentions and the true nature of the Labyrinth.

    • Ariadne’s Conflict: A peek into Ariadne’s internal turmoil, her realization of what she has done to Theseus, and her own descent into madness.

    • Resolution: The melancholic acceptance by Theseus of his fate as the new guardian of the labyrinth, embodying the creature he sought to conquer.
      6. Symbolic Elements

    • The Thread: As a symbol of connection to the old self and the outside world, and its severance indicating a plunge into self-altering depths.

    • The Labyrinth: As a living, breathing entity with its own desires and secrets, almost a character in its own right.
      7. Style and Tone

    • Cosmic Horror Elements: Incorporate Lovecraftian themes of incomprehensible realities and the insignificance of humanity in the broader cosmos.

    • Mood and Atmosphere: Build a tense, oppressive atmosphere that mirrors Theseus’s psychological descent and the labyrinth’s eerie, omnipresent influence.
