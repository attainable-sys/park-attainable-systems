exploring canary networks as a novel design space for scaling blockchain technology

A canary network is a environment that allows protocols to experiment with the stability of blockchain execution environments by allowing developers to execute high-iteration applications stress-tested against the conditions that exist in the active markets and the incentives that come from embracing a chaotic system with a higher degree of risk.

The most mature example example that we've seen at scale is the Kusama network. Kusama is Polkadot's 'cousin' canary network blockchain, providing fast-paced execution and settlement with a shared security model that allows for sharded chains to test the functions of a Substrate-based metaprotocol. (If that seems jargony, don't worry too much, it's not really the point of this article).

What this means is that people who want to release large-scale projects that benefit from Polkadot's network effect, a good way to gain exposure to the true-market conditions

So why build on a canary network? Why not first deploy on an active testnet, then launch into a mainnet standard? The answer to this question lies in exploring new design languages for how to build out software implementation - a discipline known as 'Chaos Engineering'. One of the first major entities to take an approach along these lines. All rules tend to fall away in the case that failure takes over a system - not because there are necessarily issues within the software or process themselves, but usually (and almost entirely) because of human factors.

The study of this phenomenon has lead to an expansion of the field of "Learning from Incidents" - study that concludes that a lack of coordination is where a failure insight usually emerges from - a lack of coordination over problem solving often causes the failure in the first place.

Gnosis is looking to become a canary network for Ethereum. It will provide an ideal thinking space with low finality to consider the evolution of the Ethereum post-merge ecosystem.

Proponents of this design contend that it will make the deployment of EVM projects that much easier to implement and experiment with in an active operation space and lower overall gas cost.

Sagan intends to be canary network for the Cosmos development space, a space that intends to offer locally sanctioned shared security from a permissioned set of vetted validators.

Different from Kusama, Sagan intends to offer a way for protocols native to the space to live beyond the bounds of the experimental space that it has to offer, eventually allowing them to live in the wider Cosmos space whilst still having had access to real-world asset space.

Since this design language is so early in implementation, many criticisms of the model argue that canary networks are really just an expansion on incentivized test networks, and claim that there isn't really a strong argument for the longevity of canary networks once a network goes fully live.

So what's the point?

Running a canary network has some distinct advantages, some disadvantages, but is otherwise an unproven system in the context of blockchain network scaling. Namely, it offers developers the chance to see how protocols and implementations will act 'in the wild', with real users and real incentive.

The question becomes whether or not this kind of environment is necessarily worth the effort, but the logic behind it is sound - having a space within which network designers can see how their applications will work under pressure allows ideas to incubate in a similar way that natural systems iterate.
