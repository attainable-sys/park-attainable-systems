paradigm

2222.061 · 2222 systems

A paradigm is a state of norms that exist within a system wherein the state of the system accepts these norms to be the rules of the interpretive mechanisms necessary for acting within the system.

In a scientific setting, the current 'paradigm' is the system of interpretive rules and laws that explain knowledge up to the most complete state of interpretation.

In laymen's terms, it means that the current paradigm is the most up to date 'science'. The context for this is that there were times where the information necessary to explain the world was incomplete, but those who wished to study it were interpreting the world through the tools they had available, such that those were the 'laws' of nature. When new evidence was discovered to indicate that these laws were incomplete, the weight of this evidence eventually instigated a paradigm shift wherein the previous science was seen as incomplete, requiring that the new set of rules be fully explored as an interpretive basis for the world.

Interestingly, Thomas Kuhn concluded in his 1969 book 'The Structure of Scientific Revolutions' that science undergoes similar a similar state to that of politics, where the previous science exists in a state of incumbency due to the pervasiveness of the ability for previous natural laws to explain the phenomena that we see in the universe.

The most explanatory version of this is the shift from Classical Physics (those that came about in the time of Isaac Newton) and Quantum Physics (those that came about in the time of Albert Einstein). The previous science in the time of Newton explained the physical phenomena of the universe in a sufficiently accurate way so as to 'work' for almost all frames of reference, but it didn't work at certain levels of resolution. When digging into that level of resolution, it was clear that the rules of the previous system were incomplete, spurning a need by Einstein (and many of his contemporaries) to further explore physical phenomena at this state of resolution.

The science that emerged from this period of time is what we consider to be some of the most 'accurate' science, in that is provides the most complete picture of the natural laws that explain the physics of the universe. This said, shifting over to this new form of science was met with with resistance as Quantum Physics was required to gather evidence of its accuracy, until the evidence was overwhelming and the understanding of the most accurate underlying paradigm was accepted as the new paradigm, hence causing a 'paradigm shift'.
