privacy as a trend

2100.005 · 2100 privacy
  • Gen Z seems to think that online privacy is something that really needs to be adopted, mainly because they didn't have a choice in the world that they grew up in, so are having to learn how to scale back their presence on the internet from the Dark Forest that exists when too much information is simply swilling around the internet.
  • "By moving conversations underground, young people are redefining the way they interact in digital spaces, prioritizing smaller communities and closed platforms over public arenas."

- <span class="ul" title="not published">Gen Z and The Cozy Web - Event Anywhere</span>
    - A recent survey found that 72% of Gen Z respondents are concerned about their online privacy.
    - 63% of Gen Z respondents say they have taken steps to reduce their online footprint, such as using a privacy-focused browser or deleting social media accounts.
    - 52% of Gen Z respondents say they are more likely to engage in private conversations online, such as through encrypted messaging apps or anonymous forums. [⤴️](https://omnivore.app/me/gen-z-and-the-cozy-web-event-anywhere-189a9beba0a#cbe92bac-6b3f-48ff-8b71-4d4cbcea6e6b)
    - As online platforms increasingly monetize user data, Gen Z has become more cautious about sharing personal details openly. They are aware of the potential risks associated with online privacy and are opting for smaller, closed communities or encrypted messaging platforms where they can control who has access to their conversations. [⤴️](https://omnivore.app/me/gen-z-and-the-cozy-web-event-anywhere-189a9beba0a#8295959a-af9c-4c5d-bc66-b68b93cb7c23)
- <span class="ul" title="not published">The Customer Economics of Internet Privacy. Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science...</span>
    -  To take advantage of the key benefits of online transactions such as convenience, customers need to trade off by providing valuable information about them selves--be it name, address, credit card number--all in pursuit of seamless and effortless online transactions (Feldman 2000). In fact, it may be quite impossible for customers to transact business on the Internet without revealing information about themselves that they may be unwilling to share.
