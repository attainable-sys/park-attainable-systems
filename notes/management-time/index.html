Management Time

1150.008 · 1150 management

The concept of 'feeding the monkey' is the expectation that tasks are often someone's responsibility, but a manager's role is to assign these tasks a degree of initiative and to ensure that the monkey stays on the DR's back.

The justification for this is that work on the manager's part can only happen if subordinates have ownership over their roles and responsibilities. Otherwise, ownership of tasks is constantly shifted to the manager for completion, such that the 'monkey' always ends up on the manager's back.

Source

  • Link: 'Who's Got the Monkey' - Walter Onckler

  • Core question:

    • Where is the monkey?

  • Three kinds of management time:

    • Boss-imposed time
      • required, delegated tasks
    • System imposed time
      • required, active support within working group
    • Self-imposed time
      • Manager-originated, but also taken up by imposed time from DRs (but not required)
      • discretionary time - remaining portion of a team's time.

-> The correct approach is to increase discretionary time by decreasing imposed time

"Monkey on your back" means: Whose job is it to get this task done?
-> i.e work that a subordinate needs to get done being blocked resulting in a manager taking on the task, such that the 'monkey' leaves the subordinate's back and instead is on the managers.
--> Key term then, do not let someone else make their work your job, because then you become the blocker. If you give people the agency to make decisions using the power that they have, they are responsible for the outcome and can only come to you for advice on how to do what they need to do next.

“At no time while I am helping you with this or any other problem will your problem become my problem. The instant your problem becomes mine, you no longer have a problem. I cannot help a person who hasn’t got a problem."

  • transferring the initiative
    -> Before you can transfer initiative to employees and DRs, you have to see if they have initiative in the first place.
    -> 'We' never have a problem - Only one of us does.

anatomy of initiative:
1. wait until told (lowest initiative)
2. ask what to do
3. recommend, then take resulting action
4. act but advise at once
5. act on own, then routinely report (highest initiative)

  • do not wait for someone to tell you what to do. Do it, then report how far it's gone in that initiative.

The Care and Feeding of Monkeys

  1. Monkeys should be fed or shot
  2. Monkey population should never be unmanageable. If it is, then you have to kill some monkeys.
    • It should never take more than 5-15 minutes to feed a monkey
  3. Monkeys are only fed on a schedule, never ad-hoc, and should not be chased down.
  4. Documentation does not take feed the monkey, feed it face-to-face
  5. the next feeding time and degree of initiative should be agreed upon through mutual consent and should never be vague or indefinite
