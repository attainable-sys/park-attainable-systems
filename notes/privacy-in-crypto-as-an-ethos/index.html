privacy in crypto as an ethos

2222.064 · 2222 systems

In order to teach people about privacy in crypto, there are a number of modules to be walked through - largely due to the fact that there is no unifying set of definitions that keep all of crypto in alignments. Instead, what has happened is that different crypto networks build in their own directions rather than building in a unified and collaborative dimension.

If the perspective of crypto were to be shifted in the direction of collaboration rather than competition, there's a question of building something that aligns all participants in the project of crypto so that they are all headed in the same direction for the broader mission of crypto. Most important to this is a consideration that a mission of crypto engaged with as though it is a joint imperative across all projects is the greatest guarantee that it will succeed.

If crypto is a human project, there is no reason that all of those who consider themselves a part of this project should not work together in achieving it.

Module 0 : What is privacy, really?

Privacy is about revealing, not concealing, and it is a practice of being confident that you are only sharing the information you want to share with others, not that which can be taken advantage of.

  1. What is privacy pessimism?
    1. What is wrong with Privacy Pessimism?
  2. What is privacy optimism?
    1. Why is Privacy Optimism a better practice for understanding privacy as a cultural paradigm around which people should build a sovereign identity?

Module 1 : What is the mission of crypto?

This is a question of

  1. What is the history of Crypto?
  2. What is the history of privacy in crypto?
  3. How does privacy factor into the mission?
  4. What are the stages of blockchain privacy?
    1. How does this contribute to the mission?

Module 2 : Why should you be private in crypto?

This is a question of culture - what does it look like if we have a future where we never confer privacy to all elements of a technical stack? Why is the alternative, a future that is fully private at the last stage of the stages of blockchain privacy, a better future here?

  1. How is your information vulnerable? How might it be used against you?
    - Why is this something that you should be worried about?
    - What is information leakage?
  2. In crypto, what is it that needs to be private? What are all the different parts that can be make private
  3. What is the anatomy of privacy?
    1. Different layers of crypto that feed into being private.

Module 3 : What can you do to be more private than you already are?

This section is more practical - it will be a question of what it is that you can do, technologically, in order to employ privacy practices in all elements of what you are doing.

  1. How private is crypto as is?
    - How can information in crypto be used against you?.
  2. What ways can people currently work within crypto in order to be more private?
    - e.g. CEX payments and privacy
    - composable privacy as adopted norm from the technological side
  3. Based on the anatomy of privacy, what does a crypto privacy stack look like?
    1. How should you build your stack?
      1. e.g. in Module 2 we learned all the different parts of your crypto identity that are vulnerable to information leakage.
    2. What projects can offer you different kinds of privacy across the Anatomy of Privacy?

Module 4 : How is Privacy a social good?

In this part, you teach people to take part in programs that benefit others, and that if everyone engages in privacy, then there is a practice of privacy that can be engaged with as a social good.
1. The construction of network utilities
1. these benefit all users, meaning that privacy becomes an accessible good.
2. Interoperable funding programs
1. These share value between domains, meaning that as each domain becomes valuable, there are means through which this value can support the creation of other network utilities that confer privacy (not exclusive to crypto).
3. Collaborative education is a social good
1. Coordination around resources that ensure an aligned culture of privacy moves us further along the stages of blockchain privacy
