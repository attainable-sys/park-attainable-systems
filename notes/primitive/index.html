primitive

2266.004 · 2266 technology

A primitive is a constituent module within a system that performs an atomic purpose - its use can be more widely adapted, but it has differentiated such that the utility of the primitive serves a function in the context of the wider system. Primitives, as a rule, do not change much - relative to the wider system they are a rather simple part of the network of interacting parts, and the system being analyzed cannot be broken down further than the primitive parts that make up the wider system.

However, this is not to say that the construction of a primitive is itself necessarily simple (a mitochondria is an oft-raised example of a primitive), but relative to any particular examined system, a primitive can be seen as a crucial and constituent part that cannot and does not vary much.

Understanding the behavior of primitives in the context of the wider system affords a much more clear understanding of how parts interact in order to result in order for a system to sustain itself - both in the analysis and in the design of the system. Primitives are differentiable, but not often designed for and usually a result of iteration that leads to their necessity within the larger system.
