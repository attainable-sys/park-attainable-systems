ecosystem services

2222.023 · 2222 systems

Rooted in the field of anthropology, an ecosystem service is the quality of a contingent environment that might provide some good to a system. Ecosystem services are inherently passive in nature up to the point that there is no or marginal general maintenance required in order for a system to benefit from this service - an example might be the quality of clay from a surrounding landscape and it's utility as part of building supplies, or simply having a source of fresh water nearby.
Ecosystem services are often under threat once interaction is an ecosystem passes a point of sustainability 1, wherein flow bias is one-sided and there is either no replenishment mechanism to return energy from the system to the surrounding environment in a sustainable fashion, or there is an outsized degree of inapplicable energy (non-replenishment energy) that flows from the system into the environment (a material example of this is pollution. When this happens, ecosystems tend to fall into a state of degradation wherein flow bias is entirely negative and the energy held within the ecosystem dwindles. Oftentimes this causes contingent systems to fail or reorient where they gather the energy needed for survival in the context of the environment.
