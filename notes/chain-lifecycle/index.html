Chain lifecycle

2000.015 · 2000 blockchain

Sidenote - it's an assumption that there will not be more chains in the future; it's actually likely that there will be more and that platform architecture will continue to have to evolve in response to it or lose the advantage of capital accrual. In fact, it's optimal to the market for there to be more chains since that will continue to create value across the market for all those involved, whether or not there is actually inherent value in what is being built.
There's a second layer to this, which is that as the market gets more savvy, it's likely that investment into initial development will require some degree of guaranteed utility in what the application can do.
However, this is not a rule, since there is nothing that requires a chain to keep existing past the hype that can gird the accrual of capital to those who stand to gain the most from the hype, which is inherently an attempt to profit off of a Ponzi scheme, and it's actually more likely than not that this will happen. There's not really a reason that it shouldn't.
