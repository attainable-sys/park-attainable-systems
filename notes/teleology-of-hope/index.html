Teleology of hope

1100.011 · 1100 intelligence and learning

1. Purpose

  • What problem does the idea serve?
    • I think that getting people to worship hope is what can happen in place of thinking about the loss of god and how aimless people have felt since there was this happening to humans. The problem is that people no longer have faith and I think this is something that has led to general apathy on the part of the public who could otherwise be putting themselves in a position to change things - we’ve allowed the way the world works to become entrenched, and I don’t think we need to put up with that.
  • Who will use it?
    • The general public. I don’t want to call this religion, I want to call this an ideology. I don’t know how it is that you get people to move into a state of feeling as though they can change things, but I know that it’s something important I want to see people actually end up there because I think people are sort of unhealthy right now.
  • How does it align with the world’s strategic goals and initiatives?
    • Well - the main thing is that I don’t think we’re really thinking about humanity’s goals and initiatives enough. I think we need to be looking at the critical perspective of understanding how to inspire people with health and a vivacity for living instead of lapsing into hopelessness, which is the anti-theme that exists around hope. hopelessness has an antimemetic quality that is ultimately deleterious to the idea of creating networks, and I see that hope has a high memetic quality that I think can overcome this.
    • For humanity, I think being hopeful is constructive, it’s something that serves the outcome of thinking about humanity surviving beyond their current state, so knowing this I think is what is required in order for people to better survive the capitalist hellscape that is being created around the current way that humanity is working.

2. Features

  • Requirements (which define each feature)
    • A faith of hope needs to inspire people to continue being. It needs to offer them the means to tolerate reality, and as such, it needs to
  • Context and rationale (that help explain the requirements)
    • I think a lot about man’s loss of meaning and how it is that people no longer feel as though there’s really a point to doing all the things that should otherwise be seen as useful and constructive parts of being a human being. I’d like to see that changed and I want to give people the means to have faith in a better future, both for themselves and for the world that they want to see.
    • I’m also feeling as though there are a lot way that this applies to the practice of thinking in systems, because hopefulness grounded by understanding a psychology of networks might actually create the encouraging environment that we hope to see encouraging humanity toward resolving the issues that it has created for itself.

3. Release Criteria (should cover five areas)

  • Functionality — minimum functionality required for release
    • I think that hope is something that can be easily turned into a meme in a way that will h be helpful, and there must be a way to make that work.
  • Usability — how you will ensure the product is easy to use
    • Accessibility is incredibly important here - the concept of hopefulness must be synthesizes into a set of principles that support the system in a meaningful way.
  • Reliability — how you will determine that the system is sufficiently reliable
    • Luckily, I don’t think it takes much for hope to be reliable, but I think that’s the value of having a set of principles (maybe they can be called the principles of hope or something) that can encourage people toward better self-preservation and the creation of community.
  • Performance — the benchmarks the product must achieve
    • I think there’s a question of pace here - while it can be tempting to scale this as fast as possible by trying to indoctrinate as many people as possible into a Teleology of hope, understanding the principles necessary ahead of doing is deeply important before doing so. It has to be tested in a small group
  • Supportability
    • Well - it needs probably to act as a protocol. The notion that hope is a protocol might actually be valuable enough to turn us toward the outcome that could encourage people toward meaningful growth as a species, and possibly a way for us to actually get through what it is that comes next. I’m not sure, but it feels like the right direction, at least somewhat.
