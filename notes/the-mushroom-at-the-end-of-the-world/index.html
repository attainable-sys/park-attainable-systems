The Mushroom at the End of the World

2222.082 · 2222 systems

This is am ethnography by Anna Louenhoupt Tsing framing the state of growth and adaptation that exists in the liminal spaces between where capitalism and salvage exist. She does so by outlining the experience off matsutake mushroom pickers, who tend to forests by maintaining them in a constant state of disturbance, finding that it is in these spaces that adaptation seems to unfurl for the self-contained individual.

I feel like there's so much more to dig into here, how it is that there are certain forest types that only exist because of disturbance, and that because of this it's really not really fair to engage in the concept of pristine wilderness, because this concept is in and of itself anachronistic.

Quotes:

Precarity is that here and now in which pasts may not lead to futures.

Freedom is a 'boundary object,' that is, a shared concern that yet takes on many meanings and leads in varied directions.

"Neither tales of progress nor of ruin tell us how to think about collaborative survival."

"We are contaminated by our encounters; they change who we are as we make way for others. As contamination changes world-making projects, mutual worlds – and new directions – may emerge. Everyone carries a history of contamination; purity is not an option."
