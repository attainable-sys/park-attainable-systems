society

2222.085 · 2222 systems

Economic systems are emergent properties of resource use by a society - as the complex needs of a society begin to increase, the system fragments at the level of individual contribution, and the concept of self-sufficiency becomes less important in giving way to interdependence.

In small tribal settings, this isn't true - the concept of self-sufficiency can go one of two ways - either the independent hunter-gatherer consumes their own goods (unlikely, since for the most part the act of hunting does not predicate the idea of meat preservation), or they work within a small collective to collaborate toward mutual self-sufficiency - which is far more likely. Since this is a basic aspect of social groups in any species that has the ability to hunt or gather, it's probably not a stretch to say that as a social group scales to the degree of 'society' the contributions from individuals to the whole can become increasingly fragmented, as the 'society' continues to evolve its set of needs less toward provision to the individual and more toward provision of needs for the emergent structure that depends on their input.

This is part of what is wrong-headed about how humans thing of society; society does not care for individuals when at sufficiently large scales - they contribute to an overarching structure that seeks its own self-preservation. In many ways, the writing and thinking of Ivan Illich in his approach to thinking about Tools for conviviality is the most salient - a recognition that beyond a certain scale, there are emergent qualities of a tool for coordination that will result in the tool seeking to only serve the tool.
