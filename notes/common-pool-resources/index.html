Common Pool Resources

2222.015 · 2222 systems

A Common Pool Resource (CPR) is a description used by Hardin to describe shared resources that are needed by a collective of individuals or groups that all need to utilize what the resource offers.
A good example of this is employed in describing the Tragedy of the Commons, an oft-quoted teleological description of commons management that lands on the assessment that CPRs cannot be managed by collectives and must be managed using a central authority that managed their use, else the CPR is exploited.
Ostrom rebukes this in Governing the Commons, where she describes the effectiveness of group-installed and group-managed CPRs that do not fall into a trap of exploitation by those who use it.
