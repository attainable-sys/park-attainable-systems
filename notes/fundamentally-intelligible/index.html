fundamentally intelligible

1100.003 · 1100 intelligence and learning

Fundamental intelligibility is a predicating requirement that a concept be intelligible at first pass - meaning that if someone is to understand what any given thing does, it does not require any additional effort.

A quick example of this would be the term 'heart attack' going through the process of synthesis before and after the dawn of modern medicine, with the term 'heart-attack' being itself fundamentally intelligible without further examination: a heart-attack is the act of the heart being attacked by some affliction, leading to a complete understanding of what is happening. Other examples might be 'stop sign' or 'head-phone', where there is no additional logic needed in order to understand something.
