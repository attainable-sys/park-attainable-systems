The Cruft (plot)

1050.042 · 1050 writing

In this section, the Walker arrives on the planet and moves somewhat aimlessly while trying to understand where it is, finding odd incongruence between not knowing, being alone, and the motivation to understand oneself.

In this part, the landscape is a major character and we spend most of our time understanding the unique biology of the planet under these conditions - the ways that the planet makes the Walker sick, or the ways that there is immense risk, and the reconciliation of not knowing whether or not your life has value against the innate desire to self-preserve despite this feeling.

A central theme here then can be an exploration of whether or not there is innate value to life, if it's something that deserves to be preserved, but that dying or feeling as though there is no value in life isn't a meaningful position to take, because we create meaning within ourselves.

I could also go a more basic route and write this section in a more entertaining style, thinking about what it takes create an antagonist / protagonist relationship between the weather conditions of the landscape, then the disease that it gets as a result of a fungal storm in the marshland, the possibility of death, and the realization that it doesn't actually want to die.

Maybe that's the conclusion to the book - that the Walker realizes it doesn't actually want to die because it doesn't understand the feeling of being alive or the purpose for it - something found in the understanding that there is meaning found in wanting to continue onward.

In the ideal setting, this part is an entire book in its own right, a single character who is exploring a foreign world without feeling the comfort of knowing it is in the right place, not knowing why it is there, and terrified by the burden of knowledge that already sits within its mind.
