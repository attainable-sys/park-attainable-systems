Slip Box

2222.073 · 2222 systems

Writing with a slip box is a process that acts like a funnel for information capture, allowing insight to emerge from the act of aggregating information and refining which information is important to the process of synthesizing subject matter. The outcome of this approach is to create a repository that links ideas together, allowing writing to flow freely from pre-written content and it's connections to other subject matter.

It was initially established as a thinking system by Luhmann, an archivist who used it to create connections between ideas, resulting in over 90k individual notes.

Process

There are six steps to the process of writing with a slip box:
1. Make Fleeting Notes
- Any ideas that strike you - just so long as they are documented somewhere. They don't need to be referenced, they just need to be captured. Referencing structure comes later.
2. Make Literature Notes
- Notes that require bibliographic attention and help you trace the roots of the knowledge you are looking to document. A reference system starts here.
3. Make Permanent Notes
- These are shorter notes that are used to relate thinking. Go through notes once or twice a day and think about how the information learned relates to other ideas and concepts. Exactly 1 note per idea, as a jumping off point.
4. Add permanent notes to the slip box
- File it 'near' other related notes, add links to notes that are less associated, make sure the note can be found from other places as well, whether through discussion or topic. You want to be able to 'stumble' on the subject.
5. Look through connections to a 'topic'
- This was you're not trying to come up with an idea, but rather extablishing connection in an idea you already have. Add to these connections and build our the weaker connections. Let the slip box do the brainstorming for you.
6. Flesh out a structure
7. Fill out the written connection as a draft
8. Edit and Proof read the manuscript

Ask constantly "how does this connect to what I already know" - all of your thinking must be in context.

Work to explain simply, and have everything someone needs up front.

Whitehead "Civilization advances by extending the number of important opeartions which we can perform without thinking about them."
