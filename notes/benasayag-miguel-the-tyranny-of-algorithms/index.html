Benasayag-Miguel-The Tyranny of Algorithms

2222.005 · 2222 systems

A book written by Miguel Benasayag detailing the rise of the automated whole - such that the human experience is one resulted from being directed rather than directing. Benasayag argues for a need to uncenter immediacy, since rationality has become the dominant narrative in our evaluation of action; just as we've been able to deduce the linearity of cause and effect, so must be understand that the relationship between the two is not necessarily linear and instead is comprised of a plurality of contextual probabilities that engender the outcome.

complexity here reigns over the outcome of our desire to "change the world" through a desire to seek out some sense of complimentarity in our actions to their outcomes - when in fact the only means through which we can hope to create meaningful change must be through the recognition of multiple futures that are held loosely.

Relationships to democracy

Bensayag speaks of democracy as being a process that must be defined wholly in terms of conflictuality - that we must continually orbit a moving center between beliefs. Democracy is intented the live around this conflictuality and indeed to mediate it rather than live through the immediacy of our actions and intentions, leading us to think not much further than the rationalized immediacy of our actions and intentions.
Democracy is by contrast far more conflictual - it is meant to mediate through conflictuality, so that the moving center demonstrates qualities of complimentarity rather than polarity. Polarity leads us to believe in opposites that must supercede one another, a swinging of a pendulum rather than the complex action of movement that unites either side of the swing.

Relationships to blockchain

Benasayag writes with the intention to have us understand the "tyranny" with which being directed removes our ability to be autonomous and take part in the decisions that surround us - using often the example of GPS and it's influence on our ability to conceive of time and space in meaningful terms. We are instead moved into a context of immediacy due to the automation of our transit and our actions, everything being only that which is directly before us.
