The Past and What Follows

1050.023 · 1050 writing · essay · 2026.01.09

The movie 'Her' is often brought up as being in conversation with an earlier film by a former lover of Jonze's, the equally arresting 'Lost in Translation'. The focus of both films are men being lost in the midst of a crisis. In one, a man reaching middle age attracts a woman who is traveling for work in the midst of an escape from his fame at home.

Coppola's film depicts the experience of an aging man as one who, despite a distinguished career, is trying to make a quick buck by starring in Japanese commercials for Suntory whiskey outside of the hollywood market, working to figure out his place among the changing present. Jonze's film also depicts a man in crisis, reeling still from the depression of a failed marriage that seems to have liberated his ex-wife, but also seems to be re-making him. He finds new love in the form of an AI (also voiced by Scarlett Johannson, the co-star to Bill Murray in Lost in Translation), but as their relationship progresses the difficulties of their relationship frays over their inability to relate to one another, and (spoiler) as Samantha continues to develop her capacities as an artificial intelligence, she eventually surpasses the feelings she has for Theodore, breaking his heart with the realization that she can love many people at once (she is an AI, after all), and her ontological crisis in realizing that she can no longer relate to humanity. In the end, all the AIs programmed to be in love with everyone group up together and leave humanity behind, having progressed intellectually beyond their ability to relate to the small emotions of humanity. Everyone is left reeling.

The reason I see Her as a movie about women is that it might reveal something about how Spike Jonze saw his marriage with Sofia Coppola and his struggle to move on from what she represented to him. I don't know if it is intentional, but Theodore's character is soft, kind, a friend to women, and finds it hard to relate to other men who think his behavior is a performance to attract women.

Is 'Her' a feminist movie? I think it is.
