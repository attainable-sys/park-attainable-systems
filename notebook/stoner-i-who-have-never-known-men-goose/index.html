Stoner - I Who Have Never Known Men - Goose

1055.005 · 1055 critique · craft · 2025.09.15

I think a piece of writing about Stoner, I Who Have Never Known Men, and The Book of Goose all work together:
- stoner is resigned to his station and grows a quiet fury in being trapped, even though he escaped his own
- the same is true of the girl who escapes in the dystopian novel.
- agnes responds differently by resigning herself to the station she experienced.
- her best friend is however trapped, which I'd the same fury that Stoner feels at the end of his experience
- both goose and stoner are concerned with the circumstance of war and its effect on people, while men concerns itself with the aftermath and adjustment to a long trauma.
- the question of how each character adjusts to their circumstance is where they stand most clearly as being in a state of resignation
- the binding theme is the unknown and what we use to respond to it, as well what a person can even do to escape what that response would look like- how each character adapts, the persistence of trauma, and its effects on what we choose to do with our liberty:
- The girl from Men gains her liberty from being a part of a society and from patriarchal norms, but was as well both never and always trapped within the expectation
- Agnes is liberated from her circumstances through the traumatic effects on her parents of the loss of her brother and her close relationship with Fabienne, who does not have this freedom to leave
- this echoes the sentiment of Stoner's parents hoping that he can better his circumstance by leaving behind the farm, but his parents' resignation from the trauma of their own experience as subsistence farmers results in Stoner's own resignation to what little he has available to him
- stoner experiences the loss of Dave Masters and of his students, and at a certain point he explodes into a sort of angry nihilism about their loss, nearly a desire to see further the destruction
- additionally he is chained to circumstance by his wife and her own traumatic upbringing through the experience of expectation placed upon her resulting in her own vindictive experience of self-expression, as though to make Stoner the target of her recovery
- as though to ask what was even the purpose of 'escaping'
- Stoner's daughter escapes but through horrible means due to her father's inability to be steadfast
