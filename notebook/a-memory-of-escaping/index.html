a memory of escaping

1050.032 · 1050 writing · memoir

What makes things all the more confusing was that however hard things were to navigate when we got back to the city in those months afterward, the start of it was pure and from a place that probably liberated us from anything else. It's why when we were back home, all the disagreement that led to me looking back and looking for a different way for things to have played out didn't make any sense, the context for how things started was so completely different that it didn't immediately fit within what was necessary to make it work.

When I told her I loved her, I didn't really do so directly. We were sitting on the back of a pickup truck somewhere outside of Truckee Mississippi (the irony was not lost on me) - she had turned to me and had told me that these are the sort of trips I wanted to do for the rest of my life and I found myself looking at her for the first time and seeing in her someone that had been looking at the same landscape as I had and had appreciated it through enough overlap of experience that I could see what was being stitched together. We'd parked in the empty space below a wind turbine, the whistling thrum of the blade whipping overhead - the dogs had been uncomfortable overnight but somehow we made it through their unease, and I'd woken up before dawn.

We'd driven here because the idea of going somewhere neither of us had been together but that overall was probably not that exciting of a place seemed novel -- seemed like an experience that both of us could walk way from having felt comforted by. I realize now that this is how you build relationships - you share experiences with people and you make space for those relationships to be built on the time that those shared experiences offers to each of you. The drive had taken 18 hours from Chicago; I picked her up from work with packed bags and two panting dogs and an audio book ready to go, but we didn't start listening to it until 6 hours in, so we'll have to listen to the rest on drive back, yet in the moment that we were sitting on the tailgate, the dogs milling through 4 foot grass after sounds in the underbrush (thank god for tick prevention), I didn't really want to go back anywhere. It seemed easier in that moment to think that freedom comes from thinking that you deserve a space that is entirely your own and with which you can defend yourself against the decisions of a society that are placed upon you. That truck was rebellion, for me, and sitting next to her was the same, even while rebellion is exhausting.

I was holding on to an enamel cup with a bike tube wrapped around the handle to prevent the heat from our coffee from burning my fingers, and it was as simple as you know, i think i might we might be in love in the middle of her taking a sip, her hands holding the lip up to her face, and she didn't look back - I just watched her smile slowly and close her eyes because yeah i think that might be what this is, and we didn't say anything else after that, the wind just kept whistling over head.

On the drive back, she held my hand and I felt as though I was driving back to something different from what we'd just experienced, and I didn't want to steer the car back toward Chicago. What if we just didn't go back and she looked out the window the same way that you had earlier and it would be nice to give it all up, wouldn't it?
