On healing - Kundera

1055.003 · 1055 critique · essay · 2025.03.30

I think I've been going through a period of healing. The general sense I gather is that some of this is coming from a desire to incorporate more slowness into my life, a sense of living that allows me to stop and pay attention to my current surroundings, rather than those I am examining at some undetermined period of time in a distant future. Some of this has come in the form of a moralism with respect to how it is that we engage with one another as human beings; in a networked era, we believe we must communicate with everyone who can listen to us and that every opinion is equally important, but I'm not really convinced that this is a good way of being, because it requires us to think of everyone who we can interact with as a form of audience rather than a community.
Some of the healing that I've been engaging with is a particular kind of slowness that corresponds to trying to acknowledge who it is in my life that I actually care about and with whom I want to deepen my relationships. It turns out that the number of social media followers that you have nor the number of attempts that you've made to increase that number actually give you any moral or emotional well-being. As Ed Yong put it, you should cultivate an audience, not followers.
I suppose some of this writing is an attempt to return to those who have taken on the honor of signing up for this newsletter - So far it's been something that I only release one every sometimes, but I assure you that every time I write it, I do think about the importance of having written it and the importance of why I am sharing thoughts with the people who are receiving it in the first place. This is maybe where the moralism comes into play; a sort of pseudo-social attempt to get people to listen to what I have to say as though what I'm saying has any worth and it really doesn't. Backing off of that sort of self-deprecation is a parallel thought that keeps me considering that in fact I'm not trying to convince any one of you of anything at all but rather simply trying to communicate.
I think the present era does a really good job of making us believe that all of the things we say must be somehow important or relevant or a sort of commentary on the present era, and putting ourselves in the boundary of having to express ourselves in this way, I think is mostly just exhausting and takes away from our ability to interact with one another as equal human beings.
There is a section of Milan Kundera's Book of Laughter and Forgetting where he comments on a writer speaking with a fan who walks up to him in a diner and asks for advice about how she can get into writing. He asks her why it is that she thinks she should be writing. She responds that it's possible that some people might want to read what she has to say. The author sitting at the diner bench turns to her and says, "why would you think that you had anything important to say?" I think back on this quote often whenever I start to write, because it is a form of self-deprecation - Imposter syndrome for a writer made material as though to point out that there really is no point in writing in the first place and that you should really just pack it up and go home and not even try.
I don't really think this is what Kundera was attempting to convince people of and in the years since I first read that section of his novel, I've instead found myself thinking that maybe what he's saying is that you should ask yourself critically why it is that what you are writing about is something that you want people to read. I still don't really have an answer, but I think I've stopped getting in my own way in the search for one, instead allowing the words to flow and sort of drift into purposelessness as a form of expression that attempts to connect with other people. None of us really have anything important to say. That's not the point. While maybe the author in his novel is intended as a jaded old careerist who cannot understand why he himself has written up to that point. Even his writing can be unimportant, No matter his readership. So is mine. So is yours.
This contrasts with the sentiment I've been holding around the idea that social media is something valuable in terms of how it is that we articulate our thoughts to an audience. The difference between Ed Yong's description of what fans/followers are against an audience is the difference between trying to cultivate a mob of people who you want to listen to everything you have to say versus a group of people to whom you have something very specific to say. The latter version offers people agency and the former is really just a thinly veiled attempt to self-aggrandize.
The social media profiles that fascinate me the most are those with the incredible voices who have turned off comments on any of their posts - they take distance from the work that they are creating in such a way as to understand that those who choose to read what they are working on are not necessarily people they are trying to convert, but rather people with whom they are trying to converse. Another one of Ed Yong's comments comes to mind here. "Don't argue with strangers on the internet".
Other fragments of the reading that I've been doing lately have been from authors who are reconciling a shared narrative about accelerated, networked growth as a function of technological advancement, and then those who are attempting to remember what it means to be human in absentia of this supposed progress has brought us. This latter half are those who have left behind the purported advantages of an interconnected parasocial pseudo-presence and have instead found kindness in the smallness of the world they decide to surround themselves with - people who fell into deep burnout as a function of continuing to follow the ever accelerating machine, only to be spit out and to acknowledge that we as individuals have our own needs that exist outside of the system that attempts to use us for its own purposes.
As a result of this, I think some of the healing that I am taking on or attempting to steer myself toward is reacting to a desire not to put myself into the burnout I have witnessing people (mostly men) continue to obsess over. I have over and over incorporated the "grindset" attitude to my own work and come out with next to nothing, only to realize that what was most fruitful is to focus on the most important things with the highest amount of effort in the shortest possible amount of time in order to achieve it. This sort of effort is far more rewarding because oftentimes getting into the flow of this sort of work only requires knowing exactly how you want to approach it and then putting forth the effort to ensure that you actually do. I found that this is the sort of work that comes from the product of writing out what it is that I intend to do rather than simply reacting to what I think feels like the next most important thing.
This brings me to the lesson that I feel I've been learning more recently through this period of healing and that I hope others are starting to witness in themselves because I am one to believe that we are all conduits of the society that surrounds us: we already have everything we need.
No amount of algorithmically-directed consumption is going to teach us self-worth, nor will it be what informs us of what we within ourselves deem important. If media is used as the tool, it is possible for us to reflect on what we consume against what we know ourselves, but it is more likely to put us in service of further consumption - the conscientiousness required for us to ensure that we are the vehicles of our own intention is often outweighed by the intentions of the system that feed us the knowledge we believe is informing this perspective. 'Importance' is something we have to investigate within ourselves - a thing we inquire and unpack by virtue of focused, concentrated, attentive, and most importantly loving work. It does not require this work to be continuous, but rather simply focused and attentive.

I write this out of a desire to remind myself that much of my writing is about this question of importance, but that this importance does not require others to believe that what I deem as important need be what they themselves also see as such. It also means that writing anything at all and sharing it with the small constellation of people who have chosen to read what I write doesn't require me to hold what I have to say to such a high standard as to believe that what I am saying is necessarily important rather than just a conversation.

Writing these posts, however intermittent, is an attempt to connect in a way that doesn't feel so costly through a medium that feels more conciliatory and meaningful to me in particular. If I bring you along on the journey, then that's fine, and that's fantastic, and I'm glad this conversation can be something that we are all a part of - but it doesn't oblige me to consider what it is that I am saying as somehow important in the broader sense. I don't get to decide that for any of you. I can only decide the importance relative to my own scales.

So the healing I'm taking on is to remember that what I think is important is beautiful just because I think it's important, and that I don't need to necessarily find a group of people who also believe the same thing I do because I think that's mostly what's destroyed our ability to relate to one another. If you read this and you connect to it somehow, that's wonderful. And maybe that was the point of writing, but not necessarily out of a desire to change your mind.

Maybe this writing is a form of thanks for choosing to have received this in the first place, whether or not you believed that this writing would reach you. It's to say I'm glad that you think I have something worth saying. It's to say I am glad that you are participating in my healing with me. It's to say that just possibly we have been doing things in a way that destroys our ability to find common experience and instead has put us in a position of requiring ourselves to be enraged by the continuous onslaught of information that is at once irrelevant and unimportant to our ability to live balanced quotidian lives. It's not to say that some of the more important things won't somehow reach out and affect your life directly, and I know this. But finally it is to say that yes, I do believe I am glad that you are an audience to me.

-
