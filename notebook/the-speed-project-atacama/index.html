The Speed Project, Atacama

1050.027 · 1050 writing · field note · 2024

Maybe under a year later, you will need the rest from a busy mind that cares too much about too much, and you'll help another friend make his way through the driest desert instead, and you'll find yourself asking if what you are doing is something you want to threaten yourself with doing forever. Even as you work through the edges of these different deserts with the same name, through rocky outcroppings surrounded by the sand drifting into dunes that seem miles away, you won't be sure if you have a choice.

You'll find yourself wistful. It won't be that you don't want to do this forever but rather that you don't want to hate it. You won't ever want to force yourself into reenacting something you love over and over again until it's something you don't want to do, because this has already happened so many times. It will end up being something you don't want to promise because it needs feeling sweet and memorable, - and will lodge itself in your brain like an addiction.

The friends you make in the desert are special because anyone who chooses to be here couldn't possibly be a stranger after sharing this kind of experience. You think back on running along the beach in Iquique with Nils, and hearing his honesty, and feeling mildly enraged at the way people in your past have spoken about someone who has changed as much as this human has; this human, an untouchable force that seeks out more of this, just as you do, that seeks to play with the human spirit of risk just as you do. If you have been the subject of that same envy–turned–judgment, the question then becomes: for what? For choosing to do something that others demand that there be a point in doing? But life need not be made so inflexible.

It could be described as "vacationing in hardship". There's no denying this, but if there's no point to prove, then, there is no reason to justify it. Maybe there is some form of happiness found in knowing that you must live a life in acknowledgment of how your actions affect others, but this is a complex form of happiness. It's something that comes from a lifelong practice of adjusting and respecting asymmetries. Here two things are held in tension: we must acknowledge what privilege we have to go and do what we will do, but that acknowledging a privilege does not require us to deny oneself joy. But joy is found in simple things, and running in the desert is simple. Why deny that joy?

Nicki's words will ring in your ears instead, read over his shoulder on the walk back from our first dinner on the shoreline, What if? Why not? There is more to all of these stories than trying to prove a point, so much of it is just because it's there and we want to go and see it and we want to be excited by sharing the experience with one another, and so much of it isn't to tell a story as much as the yell at the back of an RV that you fucking love this shit, because this shit is unmatched. Nothing matches an experience like this, which is why you continue to seek it out. You ask yourself - is it that much of a threat to want to go and do what you would do and have others tell about it?

This sort of "vacation" teaches you something about yourself, and it happens over and over, and you continue to learn how much you can love others just for being there as you move through an experience, whether or not that experience was a choice. It's the sort of vacation that brings back a smile and memory just because it is that much more to relish and remember the chickpea and canned vegetable rice wrap that is more delicious than anything found at a restaurant, and that hug after 25 miles being "the best hug I have ever had". As we come up with road names that change and shift with the landscape around us, our insides are stirred by the exhaustion and emotion and anxiety of deciding that this was the planned from the start, but this was a choice. It was the decision to move into that next stage of ourselves, a movement between movements. It is all movement.

It is all movement in the staccato of our show, the rain, the engine idling, the exhales and changes that cannot unchange. It is all movement that turns us into something else, and 14,000 feet up we start remembering that we are 14,000 feet up, asking ourselves why the hell we're here, but "we've already won" just by being here at all.

The movement forces us to grow, and growth was always the plan, but there have never been rules on how to follow it – each story was just to keep ourselves, doing something new, something that earmarks the distance between who we were, and who we wish to b if the weather is right, we'll share that story, but there is no obligation. All of it is one about the joy of movement and living within one's body.

Sure, challenging yourself, without a good reason may seem foolish, but again, Nicki did already say it: what if you could do it? Why wouldn't you, if you could? Asi es que se hace and Y que, si se podria? Y si se podria, porque no?
