This is what you do not already know about me

1050.054 · 1050 writing · essay

You do not know why I seek out the thin roads on the sides of mountains, nor the curl of waves that act as their cousin in the rage of an ocean. I do not know myself, but it is that to which I am compelled.

It is for this reason that I have written this as though it is a confession to you, that I am seeking out something far away, and that in the unknown I have found the need to keep seeking. I continue to traverse an unknown because have found the unknown more yielding than I was made to believe it would be. I think this much does not surprise you, but I am telling you because I am going to tell you what there I have seen. I am going to tell you why I go.

When I first arrived on the Altai, it was after 19 hours of flying and a further two days of driving a manual truck through the high plains. I did not know how to drive manual before I arrived here. A man named Ömur taught me, and I failed repeatedly in places I should not have. I drove a converted Land Rover ambulance through high plateau by myself. Ömur gave me a handgun. I never needed it, and despite Ömur's hesitance in allowing me to take the truck, he relented and in parting he hugged me, also giving me a small bottle of white liquid that smelled like fire and old egg yolk. I understand why he was fearful. I wasn't.

The first two days on the highway were an empty, strewn nothing, other than the occasional Dutch cyclist ignoring my passage. The truck was named La Pelada, taking after the journey of another radical who also left home and became someone else. I ate stale bread, cooked tea, stewed lamb, and thought of everything else. I was the happiest I'd ever been, just like every other time I've lived out of a vehicle somewhere else. I missed my dog. The chatter of the road was enough a constant that the progress was slow. I had to accept this being true, and it was, and even when I thought a rock had pierced the old body of the chattering Pelada and an old Hilux stopped to make me wish I hadn't left the handgun in the dashboard, they helped me. I never felt as though I wouldn't be helped. This was not the first time I was helped.

I must pause here to have you understand. I know that in order to love this place (and so many others) I have to have the freedom to leave wherever I am. I don't believe in traveling as a way to tour the world through my privilege. I believe I am learning something. I do not think I am finding myself in the dust and spit of fixing a broken axle on a sidelined truck for a stranger named Holün, who thanked me in broken English with good, good, Obama, a thumbs-up, or in wearing grease from that truck for several more days before I found a soap strong enough to remove it. I believe that I have this freedom because everyone desires to have this freedom, and I seek out those who are happy because happiness exists everywhere. Yes, I breathe in the privilege of knowing I can leave while some poor Kyrgyz man could save forever and never leave Bishkek, but I also know that some of these poor Kyrgyz men and women are happy, because happiness is easy to find. Yes, they want more. Everyone does. Only some have access to the means. I am one of these people. But it does not do right to imagine people in hard conditions incapable of joy. It does not do to believe that suffering is a form of purity. It does not do to reduce the experiences of people whose lives are simplified by a lack of what we define as privilege into experiences of dismay. They are taken advantage of my people who want more than what they need.

I do not believe my desire to meet them should somehow condemn my movement through their landscapes. I think we have broken something within ourselves in not being able to honor the lives of others. We diminish them by holding them pristine, and people who live in these places have lives. I have been to the films with them in a small town that only showed a film I didn't know anything about in a language I didn't know. It was one of the funniest movies I've ever seen, even to this day.

I pause because in telling you that I am learning something, I am telling you that I am learning how to appreciate that which we already have. I seek out those mountains because they are older than we are and in the places where we humans lack the hubris to tear off their tops in search of gold, they can teach us more than we could ever seek to learn.

On that highway I drove for three more days. It was all I had time for, I could have left for longer - Ömur had only taken 100 USD down as a deposit, and there was otherwise no way to guarantee we would make it back. I thought about stealing it. You know I wouldn't do that, but still spend 8 hours of westward skittering over gravel thinking about driving it to the border of Tajikstan and further south to where my friends could hold it in Thailand. But Ömur offered me his faith, a bottle of mysterious white liquid, and his gun, as though it was the easiest thing in the world. It is so easy to defect from the agreements we make with others. It is as easy to hold ourselves to them.

I turned back once I reached a Monastery on the 5th day. I don't remember its name, but I have a photograph of it that I printed out in order to make sure I remembered it. It was a Christian monastery. I slept in the apse or their church. The vault of the ceiling was as beautiful as every other church I have found peace in. Driving into Bishkek on return, Ömur joked that he was worried his wife would hurt him for chasing another woman across the mountains. I gave him back his gun with a smile across his face. He said that it was a symbol and he had kept me safe. I expect that he did, in a way.

That drive back from the monastery was too short, despite the distance being the same in the grand loop; it is often this way, palpable that we are forced into a new expectation of what must be next instead. This is the privilege, to not have to sit and remember that there are people you have just met. I remember their smiles and the permission I was given to keep them with me in the form of photographs. I don't know if I'll ever have the chance to remember them any other way because in a time that seems to accelerate our use of memory, I fear that if we never get the chance to stand still and remember over and over, the joy of these moments leaves us. I drove back and on the last night had to drive in the dark, frustrated by the dark ocean I didn't get to witness rising around me. I had time to eat dinner with Ömur. I left the next day.

This is what you do not already know about me. It is more and more than I can give words to. It's more and more than I have ever tried to, but I feel I must. I must because I am desperate for you to know what it means to love a planet so completely and yet be so complicit in knowing that I will never be able to have you understand what I know. I need you to know in the same way that I must know you, that I am desperate to know you as well. I fell in love with you at the first sight of knowing that I could.
