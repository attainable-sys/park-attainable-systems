Memory palace

1100.002 · 1100 intelligence and learning · essay · 2026.01.27

There is a way of remembering things called a 'memory palace'. It functions by taking a concept that one might seek remembering and setting aspects of what you seek to remember into a hypothetical physical space. For many people, the reason a 'palace' can function is because palaces have many hallways to walk through, but if one has enough the ability to conceptualize, it is possible to conceive of these hallways and their connections, offering one the ability to mentally traverse through the construction in order to see the connections between a concept otherwise difficult to remember.
If this is true, New York City is my memory palace, and the thing I am constantly remembering is all the ways it has broken my heart.
Winter in New York City is invariably difficult. If you have a normal job, it's a time that cascades forward from of the urgency of End-of-Year Reviews, the anxiety of unfinished work or a perceived lack of progress, a week or a few days of pause (whether taken or not) -- directly into the blank expectation of the a cold, gray new year. The only time New York is truly beautiful in the winter is when it snows, but specifically when it snows the sort of storm that buries cars to their windows. It is the only time that blank expectation of the new year actually looks like something to work with.
Shortly thereafter, the snow starts to melt, the grey ponds of sludge at the heels of each block become obstacles. If you're lucky, it stays cold enough that instead you are forced into queues by walls of snow on either side in an attempt to the cross the street. I love New York City in Winter.
The last time it snowed to this point, I remember people calling it a 'Snowpocalypse'. This was around 2014, I had just moved to the city, I was just over a year and a half out of college. I'd met the first love of my life. I was riding a bicycle everywhere in the city. It was the first time I heard the expression 'brick'. I learned it from a Facebook comment. The most recent time it snowed this way was this year.
The intervening years brought nothing like this - none of the frozen river that accreted strange circular floes, nor the level of cold that made me respect the latitude of this city. I ran in shorts the entirety of the winter of 2024 into 2025. I remember thinking that the world was ending. When it snows in New York and they suspend Alternate Side Parking for weeks at a time, I can fool myself into thinking otherwise.
The intervening years also brought me a sort of respectful understanding of a city that seems to take pleasure in making things difficult. I have had two careers here, and both have been successful. I have loved three women in the time I have walked, run, taxied, and bicycled across this city. Maybe four. I remember all of them by the places I pass by years after those loves ended. I'm reminded of the hurt constantly. New York has a way of reminding you to stay humble, and the shape of memory is in the buildings that don't disappear, until in some deep level of surprise you see dark green cladding wrap itself around one of your favorite pre-wars, where the Bulgarian man used to sit on the stoop every week, cigarette in hand, with a new Belgian Malinois he was training at his side. Full demolition. It never happens anymore, just like the snow never asking you to posthole your way to the closest grocery store because your hubris made you think you wouldn't need to replace the toilet paper after the storm.
One of the other facts of living in New York is the acute awareness that everything is happening around you, all the time. Even in January. The anxiety of feeling trapped in the mire of grey sludged winter in New York is the reason people leave when it gets cold. That history of the 'snowbird' started here. Miami wouldn't be what it is without that snow. Anyone who is too poor to head south or to ship their skis to Aspen instead is subjected to the anxious pell and uncomfortable mell of created expectations, of setting oneself up for what we expect to come next.
Meanwhile, this winter, I was unemployed. It's the longest I haven't worked; I careened into burnout in my second career after not feeling like there was a point to the first, and that snow that finally fell feels like the blank sheet I need to think about the third. Resting hasn't been easy for me, but it's given me time to think. Depression is a symptom of needing to recuperate from trauma. I extracted myself from a career that was sucking the soul out of the intentions I had for myself. I came out of it on top, but it left me listless. The palace of this city is the number of times I, while bundled to the gills with my giant black dog, have run into my ex walking with her partner-now-fiancé is as though this city doesn't want me to forget that I was taught something. Eleven years since the last time it snowed. I am a different person than I was then. Only partially for the better.
