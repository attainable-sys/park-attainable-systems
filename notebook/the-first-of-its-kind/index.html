The first of its kind

1000.002 · 1000 living and loving · journal · 2023.07.03

The first of it's kind - moving through all manner of space toward whatever if meant to come next, at whatever pace is meant to come next, and yet there's so much unexplained, so much left up to guessing, so I guess I'd be better off just believing that there's nothing wrong with being in between states and recognizing what and where things need to come from. What nuance do you find in knowing? What silence is found in keeping quiet?

There's so much more in this second core, I haven't been able to pin it down, the priority of thinking beyond current conditions and believing, however blindly, that you are fully in charge of what comes next. That's manifestation, that's the rhythm that was heard in between silences. I led myself here. I led myself toward the practice of self-becoming, and I'm all the better for it.

And yet, somehow, I've had to tolerate what it means to have to exist in an in-between state. I've had to tolerate the acknowledgment I have of having had to think in between things and postulate what has to happen next. I'm not tired. I'm not thinking about what it takes to get to the next part of myself. I'm simply moving there.

There's something huge in that feeling - knowing that I have made happen that which needs to happen next.

So what does it mean to have to feed into the pressure of what it is that, surrounding you, believes it deserves to take your energy? There's nothing less intimidating than knowing that you are in control of silence. Those are just words, and there is nothing grueling about having had to navigate them. It's a special flavor thinking and having had to think - of moving through becoming and not having had to think too hard about it.

What good does it do to have to think that far ahead? So far all of our gut instincts have been correct, and nothing could be done with it. No one was able to show us whatever it was that had to happen next, we figured it out. Somewhere in all of that self-navigating was the understanding that the higher self somehow exists around and beyond the experience of the living individual, the version of ourselves to lives in the present and is forced, continuously, to adapt to its surroundings. Aren't we better off knowing that we have made this life for ourselves? I know that I am, at the least, that I made it here and we decided to be in this place together.

What good does it do to think otherwise? What kind of frustration is really suppose to come from having taken on everything else, and what good does it do to have thought that the time spent could have been spent more wisely. These are all the things that no one has told us to learn about, so I think we're better off for having learned what good can come from paying attention.

I think it's not unreasonable to assume that the adoption of a positive mindset is a network phenomenon that requires we attend full well to the passage of time. There's much more here than there should be, that's the thing. There's much more here than there should be, and we need to take into account that thinking in this way is something that you cultivate, so it's something that you have to take seriously. The act of self-becoming is a glorious one that takes intention, it takes working on knowing your bearings. It does not mean that you have to think too hard about where you are going, you simply need to keep becoming. be real, son, be real as though some sort of mantra that I know I've heard somewhere, and I'm not trying to hold on to it, it's just there - what good does it do?

So let's keep it in motion and at the best of times, we'll have learned that there's a hell of a lot more here waiting for us than we ever thought there would be, a sense of alignment that comes from running deep through the middle of ourselves, from being the single electron kicked into motion by the passage of time, what good does it do to think otherwise?

And for one, I know that there's time to think about the future. I'm just not angry about the past being something that we have to let go of.
