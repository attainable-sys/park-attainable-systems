The Two Types of Internet

2266.001 · 2266 technology · essay · 2023.12.20

a bit of a ramble on the direction I think the internet might be headed. I think we'll see two things. 1. Consumer internet most people will spend their time here, but generally speaking it will lack in much social depth. I think in part this will be the realm of the creator economy and adspace, where the goal is to make things that people want, or to find things that you want. It will mostly be a domain of parasocial interaction or direct consumerism, and will orient itself mostly around good user experiences and not much user input. It is generally not interoperable because it is a domain where platforms compete for market share, and the best way to do so will be to silo interactions into walled gardens. Users will generally not contribute their thoughts and opinions here, and the computer literacy will stay low in general. General examples: Instagram, TikTok, Pinterest, YouTube, Snapchat, etc. 2. Community internet These will be smaller corners of the internet where people who are literate in how to use all different manner of communication will create community across any number of platforms; the social layer will act (acts) as an interoperability mechanism since those who are in this space take their time to build relationships with one another, agnostic of medium. The barrier to access will be some degree of internet-native computer literacy, both socially and practically. Tech that is used here is a means to an end, and those involved will always be communities of intention. It's from these communities that new social coordination mechanisms will form that can operate on a digitally local level that does not care about the limitations of meatspace. More "interesting" things will be made here that generally will work to bring people closer to ideals Examples of this kind of internet are most of crypto and true FOSS, some science communities, Farcaster, Mastodon, and many subject area Discords and Subreddits, and will work best when they do not aim for popularity but rather functionality, since too large of anything becomes operatively diluted in utility. -- Both of these are ultimately good things, and characteristic of humans slowly evolving back toward tribal tendencies while using technologies that we made but did not have the social tools to adapt to. A consumer internet could result in people spending less time online, since they realize that they are not truly interacting with others and will spend more time interacting in meatspace, but it's equally likely people will doomscroll these environments. My hope is that socially we'll evolve past this, but that's just optimism. The countervailing force could be a heterogenous Community Internet, where select people who want to find their people can move with intention toward those who hold similar ideals, and will make meaningful things together. These communities will be small-bit-mighty, and will likely have enormous areas of effect on a Consumer Internet, since it will be a place for people to organize. I could also foresee competition between these internets, mostly due to a Consumer Internet wanting eyeballs and despising the organizational effects of there being spaces to organize online (this, I think, is actually what the purchase of X was about). -- Time will tell, but I think the greatest differentiating factor between the types of people that will occupy these internets will be based on the many forms on literacy, sociality, and collaborative tendencies. People will occupy both spaces in many cases, one out of obligation to a society that has integrated internet-as-media and the other out of a desire to find our tribes - but I hope that this works broadly in our favor. Generally, I think it will.

Crucial to this working is developing a sense of what online culture can look like in its many facets and how people can learn to navigate online spaces as either newcomers or as collaborators. Some communities will operate privately and only by invitation, while others will be inviting and places where others are attracted to feeling included - but I think all of this will evolve out of a human desire to create communities around mutually assured survival. I think there is a high potential that a good Community Internet might work toward better coordination despite individual differences in belief, in stark contrast to the feed-based internet that we had in the Social Media era that amplified discontent bringing us back to a time when humans simply dissociated from one another in situations of discontent. It will be difficult to determine whether or not it's working - tribes can still go to war with one another, after all - but maybe we'll have just a few less reasons to oppose those we're in community with than we do when faced with a feed of literally every other person on the planet.
