The effort to be made

1000.022 · 1000 living and loving · journal · 2026.06.27

The effort to be made, the effort to be made, the effort to be made. I do not need to get up at 5 in the morning to write, then grind away my life. I'm not really interested in being the sort of person who needs to show everyone else what I do and how I do. June is the ninth month, it is the halfway point, and it is the point at which it's all going to turn around. I'm not sure what will happen over the next 6 months, but I think I can make it good.

The question I keep asking myself is what I want to play. I have not focused on this question enough — I do not seem to know what the end character looks like, to start with.

She is strong. I know this much. Her time at the gym is about having the best body she can, on her terms of life. She does not worry about money — not because she makes tons and tons of it, but because she has her finances managed to a point that there isn't much to worry about — things like big dinners might raise an issue here and there, but the baseline will always be safe. She dresses well every single day because it is important to give people the impression that she takes care of herself and that impression's effect on others. People come to her for advice, and it's part of her job — the work of being someone who lifts others up, making them into the best version of themselves. She is not tied to any one thing, but she is very good at what she does.

Now in first person: I will direct people on policy contribution. I'll be an expert in the sector because it is crucial, and because it is an area I can talk about for a long time. I will write about this. I will talk about this. I will work on this.

I will also be a writer. Not just opinions on, for instance, energy policy, but art and fiction, or on subject matter and its relevance. I will work on contracts, and I will have to travel to examine facilities and how they function in line with expectation broadly set.
My intellect makes my abilities, and I will do the work of translating for others what they do not know or understand.

I find fulfillment in the work because it makes me feel that we are driving toward a better future for everyone at hand. I will match this energy. I'll take charge of conversations because I am the sort who knows how to navigate them. I like control. I want to do more of it. It is a problem I have: how to work on, and a problem I want to be in there for. I know myself in this way.

In the mean time, in my life, I am still writing. I am doing it differently than I used to; now I work in a way that suits my love of the discipline, rather than fleeting as though I have to perform for those that are overly focused on numbers and on impressing others. My sense still continues to be that the direction of athleticism is in the direction of adventure. People want embodiment — and while we are moving in the direction of embodiment — we will praise those who do the work to make the pursuits remarkable — who push beyond the expected.

This means I write about two things: endurance, and energy. I can write about either under the other, and part of the reason that I do has less to do with the environments I am a part of: I know these things because I am already doing these things. I am a part of them because I am a part of them, just as I will be in the future. This is an invigorating fact.

The version of myself that exhibits these qualities already exists: I am her. I haven't found a way to be in the position I need to be yet, where what I do is what I will be doing, but I feel a certainty for something I need to try: there is something there I want to explore.

The person I am is known because I do not mince words and because I am an advocate. I know what I am talking about when given the chance to speak, and I speak confidently. I am the difference between a leader and a boss — someone who inspires, but not someone who requires themselves to manage people on anything more than a mentorship level. I do not put myself in such a position, but others want me there, more often than not.

Let's summarize, then: I make probably around $300K a year, but I do not all come from one place. I dress well every single day, without fail, because it is how I want people to perceive me. My personal brand is affable, confident, connected, and attentive. I care about everything that surrounds me. I am determined. The work that I do takes me to exciting places because that is where the work needs to be done, but also because I want to be there. People want me there because my being there means something to what is happening. My presence will mean to the people involved that they are working on the right thing.

The question then becomes — how do I take where I am right now and position myself so that I get to be this person? What story do I need to tell about this in order to become her?

My career is not confusing. The throughline is essentially obvious: it is energy, and it has always been energy. My fascination with the world is factually rooted in this knowledge, and I don't need to doubt this fact. It is the foundation I am and have been building on.

      • Academics

    • abeyance is the act of not immediately drawing a conclusion, and ambiguists are well-suited to being the people who maintain a position of skepticism around subject matter in order to critically examine how something fits into society

OPEN

  • something really exciting is happening in running, want to talk about it
  • but to get there: how trends rise and fall
  • credentials: Park, 14 years NYC running scene, anthropology degree, started as photographer w/ OSR, shifted to participation

THE BOOM

  • running blew up out of COVID — 27 million new/returning runners 2020-2022
  • run clubs everywhere
  • people wanted to be runners — brands and institutions figured that out
  • Marathon Majors proof point: original five in 2006, Tokyo added 2013 (seven years), then Sydney AND Cape Town in just the last two years, ninth on the way
  • that's not about the sport needing more marathons — that's profit

THE VIBE SHIFT

  • you're feeling it, I'm feeling it
  • two forces hollowing it out:
  • commodification (top-down): community is why running works → companies/institutions stripped that for promotion and money → races got too expensive → people asking what's the point
  • dilution (bottom-up): the thing that felt special when it was yours and your crew's starts to feel like everyone's, then no one's
  • the sport was never the problem — the culture got hollowed out

THE PIVOT — ULTRA-ENDURANCE

  • huge shift away from talking about training for the majors
  • attention moving to adventure-style ultra-endurance
  • Dowdle: 306 miles, 73 hours, G1M Ultra — 4.2-mile loop every hour until everyone else sat down
  • people are drawn to efforts they can't easily imagine themselves doing
  • marathon boom = participation ("you can do this too"), this = aspiration ("how far can this go")
  • can't commodify a hundred-mile race through the mountains

THE CRESCENDO — WOMEN

  • who's leading this shift? women.
  • Dauwalter: just ran Cocodona at 41
  • Entrekin: won Cocodona outright — 56 hours, 19 min of sleep, overall course record. "I feel fine."
  • Western States this week: deepest women's field ever. Abby Hall back. Molly Seidel's first 100-miler. Jenn Lichter's Black Canyon course record. International field. Men's defending champ and runner-up both declined to return. The women's story IS the story.
  • Vol State 2018: I watched Rhoda Smoker come in second overall across 314 miles — eight years ago. The gap has been closing. People are just now noticing.

CLOSE

  • running isn't dying, it's correcting
  • the culture is shifting from "can you do this?" to "how far can this go?"
  • land on women + this moment
