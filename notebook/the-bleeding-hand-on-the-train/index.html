The bleeding hand on the train

1000.019 · 1000 living and loving · field note · 2024.05.04

I watched a man's hand bleed on the train while wedging a book beneath his arm, the hand kept bleeding and he held an old T-shirt to it, and I kept thinking 'that shirt is ruined unless he gets it under cold water' while feeling waves if self-conscious fear just rampant coming from him, his bright red face when he got on because it had just happened and I'm sure it hurt but all of us hold back our pain when we feel as though we need to show others that we're capable of dealing with it.

I dropped the red-lead pencil I'd found on the side of the street right on its point and I kept thinking about how 'the core's probably broke now' so 'i'll just ride it out until the edges curl out' all the while listening to a woman caterwauling a version of a popular song that no one clapped to when she stopped, and the bare disgusting feet of a homeless man lain across the seats and no one sitting next to him, but he just wanted to sleep and I think that's fine and he should have been allowed to, but where are his shoes?

it is true that there is a dense sense of loneliness that all settles around being a transgender person who has assimilated sufficiently into the rest of society such that they no longer stand out for these parts of themselves. this can take the form of having to profoundly assimilate aspects of the self such as one's voice or mannerisms or dressing or the surgical alteration of the body to meet the standards of a society that would wish transgender people not to exist and yet somehow inside of this there is an understanding that there is profound loss in aspects of the self when reflected entirely off of a society that has not been made to apprehend any deviation from it's central norms.

I can feel the small sense of desperation that comes from realizing that there are some who have taken unto themselves a strong appreciation for that which makes them unique rather than tossing aside these aspects as though they are not something that makes them stand against the course of normalcy.

I deserve a love that sees me for who I am.

-
