TSP - the religion of running

1025.001 · 1025 the speed project · field note · 2024.04.02

What a trip.

Flying into Las Vegas with only the slightest understanding of what to expect, easing in to understand the dynamic, then feeling everything within me given to the road as a way of communing with the religion of running - i'm beyond astounded by what lives in us human beings.

We are capable of so much, of building out our bodies as vehicles for movement that seek only to commit to the outcomes that are necessary. In so doing, the act of movement becomes something that brings us up into ourselves and unfold us in the direction of a higher self, a transcendent state of committing to the ways we can push ourselves through the kindness of the relationship we build with our bodies.

I feel as though so much grew out of what we did out there. I've done things like this before but for some reason this really did feel different and was definitely born out of a need to grow in the direction of my own care and the love I have for the women that I run with. Even seeing that Julia had a really different attitude toward running as a result of this gave me a lot of grace around what I've built with them - not to mention that there's a shotgun of feelings I have about the people I did this with.

I want to sit with what Lauren said about Tam, but I don't know if I feel some strange sense of manipulation that sits around the edges of my feelings there, a desire to know and feel more about knowing if I'm actually holding Tam in my life in a way that is respectful of my boundaries and what I need for my wellbeing. I get the sense that it is and that for now it doesn't feel bad, but I do question what it's like to have my apartment back to myself, or have a roommate who actually pays rent, idk.

Beyond this I also question the fact that she helps in ways that are substantial but that I also know she really can't pay rent in the ways that she would like to. There's some martyred part of myself that flares up here around thinking that she's destined for something greater and that it's because of this that she's someone I want to harbor in my home. I don't know if this is a real feeling or if it's something that I need to look at with a degree of skepticism for my own well-being, because I'm actually not sure if I feel good about the way I'm giving myself to the situation. Some part of me would actually love to have the apartment back to myself, so I want to listen to that part and let it grow into whatever it needs to while also knowing that I appreciate the relationship we have for what it is and what we make from it, because I cannot believe that which we have made from it.

What Lauren said about feeling unthanked is valid, and I do at times feel as though she's someone with vision that deserves to be followed - however it is interesting the number of times someone has asked us why she is doing it and if it was for a cause, and it really wasn't, and probably won't be, but that simultaneously it is because it's her story of being able to exist in these spaces as someone who as of recent history didn't and doesn't have the liberty to have grown through privilege, but I don't know for sure how or why to feel anything about it because I'm just sort of allowing it to happen.

This last part feels like the crux, actually - I don't necessarily feel bad about the fact that I'm allowing it to happen and I do feel sufficiently checked in on this questions, at least as far as I can tell.

Separate from this, reading about Hunter Schafer is something that feels really grounding around the knowledge of how I approach being trans - because it's just not something I want to have to hold onto in the ways that I'm forced to. In the article she did for GQ she speaks to this idea by saying that:

there’s a meta quality at play here that we’re both aware of; of having to acknowledge the thing in plain language so that the thing might not have to be acknowledged one day.

which I find wildly true particularly because she is someone who has really made things happen for herself and doesn't want to be typecast into an identity that needs to encapsulate this as an experience. So many trans people allow themselves to end up in this pace that acts as a reverberating echo chamber of sadness and frustration, and I just can't sit in the feeling of knowing that it's something anyone should continue to have to live within in order to continue being.

Being trans is incidental, it's not something that people should have to continually face in their lives because they should be allowed to be complete humans beyond the point of self discovery, and what many cis people seem not to understand is that there's really a need for progressing beyond the trauma you had to move through in order to leave behind your experience and develop into a complete human. It can come off as being stunted, as being somehow connected only to this part of ourselves that happened to us, and as a result is absolutely an act of living in one's own trauma rather than developing beyond it.

My journey has absolutely been unfolding in the direction of something greater, so knowing that Hunter Schafer has been able to embody this same limitless energy is proof positive of how I'm approaching things, that I'm far more complicated than this single facet, even though it appears in my life over and over again and I am forced to give it attention. It doesn't define me, which is what makes it a separate experience from what I actually feel about who I am and what I want to do - that again, being trans is incidental, not a defining feature of who I am.

It doesn't mean I don't want to advocate for this part of myself or for others - I also know that I have an intense and willing attraction to other trans people at least in the regard of fearing that sort of connection - (most and foremost I think of the bartender at Rialto Grande who I always flirt with, which is refreshing because we both know and yet love to toe that line, just because it's the thing to do).

I think my relationship with running has made itself into something that lives on top of this, as well - that I'm a woman athlete and that there are parts of who I am that don't show up the same as for the experiences of cis women. However I actually find this to be something I hold a lot of promise around; mostly I feel as though I'm on par with the women that are a part of my cohort and who do the things that we do together, and mostly this is because I envision that there's something substantial to the reality that surrounds it - we're all similar and we gravitate toward one another because of the ways that we are similar, and yet somehow I do still see that we have our unique qualities that come to the fore around it. We are all impressive as hell as runners and this is something to be immensely proud of, and I think that the story of us as women who are doing these things makes this even more true.

This ties up my identity as a woman with that of other women athletes and the meaning that necessarily shows up around being in these spaces, because I remark so often on the fact that because I don't center my transness, I am not centered for my transness. The world responds in kind to that which you hope to seek from it, so if you choose to mute some part of yourself based on the way it makes you feel, this is fine, this is something deserved and that should be celebrated because I shouldn't have to live my life as though I'm required to take on these parts - in fact I know I don't.

That's why being in these spaces like the The Speed Project afterparty felt so magical - I no longer think or fear these things as being something that I have to defend, because I know that I don't have to. No one has a right to asking more from me with respect to the feeling that I hold around it, nor does it have to inform my interactions. It's entirely likely that I was the only trans person at that entire party, but I didn't even think about it, which frankly astounds me more than anything else.

My relationship with my womanhood is more important and more grounding than this, and most of the time I laugh at the fact that there's ever a part of the world that gets it wrong, largely due to the energy that I'm willing to put out and off and into to the world. I think this is magical because it's like transmuting elements - knowing so strongly something about myself that I don't need people to have to affirm it anymore (even though yes, 6 years in I still want that affirmation more often than not).
