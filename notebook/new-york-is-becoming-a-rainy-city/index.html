New York is becoming a rainy city

1000.007 · 1000 living and loving · field note · 2024.04.03

New York is becoming a rainy city - a place that doesn't look like it used to, doesn't act like it used to, and won't be prepared for how it's going to be in the future - more like Seattle than what it used to be, no longer a biting temperature of winter closing and more of the form that seems to want to piss down constantly and that none of us are actually prepared to put up with.

it's funny because this is the time of year where I used to sit outside of coffeeshops for hours and hours in the mornings, somewhat reading books, somewhat reading my own mind - and this doesn't really seem possible anymore. I don't necessarily feel poorly about it, rather it's something to adapt to, something that is changing and that is going to have to lead to a new outcome.

being on vacation is it's own feeling, too - knowing full well that I don't actually need to do anything feels a little weird because it feels like admitting to myself that I don't actually need to do anything in the mean time of having decided not to do anything. I have to let myself acknowledge and let go of the feeling of thinking that I am required to come up with things that I have to do and that I need to be productive around. I don't - even if I go upstate with a guitar, a couple changes of clothes, food, books, and my notebooks and I come out of it not having done anything meaningful - that's actually fine, because that is somewhat the point of doing nothing: relaxing into a state of finding oneself in and among that which if makes us up. I'm excited by this prospect and it's something I want more of, to find peace in myself that I already have and to have that love for myself because this is something that I want out of the situation. I want to go up there and read and explore my own mind (should I bring mushrooms? maybe I should!) but the point can and will be to be with myself.

I never know who I am writing for, in fact - I used to write as though there was some audience that would read what I had to say, and I think I've moved away from that somehow, mostly out of a feeling that there is more to my own mind that deserves exploring and respecting, and I'm trying to start from this place rather than anywhere else.

Maybe at some point when you are writing for other people, you start to hear your own voice coming through whatever is sitting within it. When and if this is true, the deliberation placed into the words becomes apparent and there isn't really a reason to reject the notion of having to mean more to the words than you want to - of using the words as a form of practice rather than as a form of outward expression.

The question then becomes about crying, about feeling ripped from oneself and respecting the boundary condition that exist around the self and the expansion of the mind. Is the act of writing for others the act of telling people what you are thinking and feeling, or is it the act of arguing for a changed state of being?

If the latter, the writer has a responsibility to the reader not to tell a story, but for this story to change their mind - to present the truth as incontrovertible and then to controvert - because the Original Story is one that follows a known thread only to end up far away from it - un hilo guiando hacia otros caminos.

For the reader to come to the novel as their full selves is itself a sublime act, for a writer is required to reconcile that they arrive with the weight of their experiences and that this informa their response to what is written. As such that which is written and carried to the reader is a deeply vulnerable act of facing a stranger and telling them, earnestly
