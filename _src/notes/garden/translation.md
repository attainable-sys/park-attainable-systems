on translation and the [[search for meaning]]

The books that she felt I had to read, the parallels. Translation - the action potential of how translation is the act of trying to approximate feeling, that the repetition of words has its own weight, or that this weight should not be lost in the search for explanation.

What is 'Man's search for meaning?" - the question [[Inma]] posed of me, while we were somewhere on the parkway, _what is meaning?_ and my answer, a seeking after the explicit, but as well a resonance, those moments where it is possible for words to arrive at least fairly close to experience. What is meaning if not the desire to reflect in reality some sense of approximation to the models that we create in our minds?

Meaning, to this degree, is a human endeavor - a metacognitive process of eventualizing reality into a set of concepts and terms of explicit nature, but those that notice that it is incomplete are those that see beauty in this disagreement. The overlap of language as a toolkit for modeling reality humors that languages do not quite match up when we try to translate between them - that in some languages terms like _élan_ or _va y viene_ cannot translate well because the feeling that they describe is one that comes from a context of developed contingency. This is the beauty of humanity's desire to describe all that surrounds them - that they never will.

It's easy to see then that poetry is beautiful because it accepts the roughness of language as an incomplete tool for the outcome - Heisenberg marries Barthes' _punctum punctillium_, the fly in the ointment that makes for the most beautiful of appreciations, those that iron in enrapture by caressing the audience rather than forcing them toward meaning. Poetry is a dance with the inefficiency of words.

Appreciating this, we can see translation itself as a form of poetry, chiefly because translation demands of one the ability to approximate the meaning of a text and the manner in which it is delivered -- Kundera's deep aversion to his translators makes sense of the frustration that he felt in seeing that his feeling was not carried over between texts - many such cases. Bad translation attempts only to find correspondence between languages, where good translation seeks out the experience of a reader in order to anchor them in this experience. This is exceedingly rare.
