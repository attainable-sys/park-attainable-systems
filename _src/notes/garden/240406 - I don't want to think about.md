I don't want to think about this too hard. I initially thought it would be interesting if there was an alien species that humans somehow interacted with, but I'm realizing that the theme of hubris is much more attractive to me in light of a humanity that believes itself to be more important than it actually is. A world where humans came up with a true miracle material that produces its own energy might be interesting, only to realize that it was actually consuming the energy of space all around us because we didn't actually understand how it worked, so as it began to do so, maybe water did actually become the most important resource because it's made up of hydrogen and oxygen, so the steady realization was that anywhere that had carbon, water, and oxygen in abundance began to dehydrate

Why wouldn't a civilization that has this miracle material run away from earth at the dirst chance to do so? It feels like this is something I have to consider - but it might be the reason that they can't actually leave earth, because they don't completely understand the qualities required to produce energy - maybe what happens is that the material ionizes the specific radiation of organic creatures for some reason, and because of this there's a degree of risk in using it as a dependable material, *but* this allows for the idea that cities can exist where the energy required is based on the sheer biomass of humans all gathered in one place.

[[the loneliness of hubris|Hubristic loneliness]] - Thematically this is something that I want humans to experience in the universe I'm creating, or rather for it to be something that the Walker is dealing with - a complex realization that humans overstepped their boundaries as a species and as a result destroyed the one place that could have been valuable - this is where [[Hayelek]] leaves their quote about humanity off.
