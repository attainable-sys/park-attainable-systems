## The Book in 3 Sentences
In this book, Kuhn argues that incontrovertible evidence is what causes the shift between interpretive frameworks for the description of systems. Previous [[paradigm]]s remain in a state of [[incumbency]] with the weight of their evidence, but only as long as the evidence that supports them is more compelling than the evidence of a new [[paradigm]] of interpretive laws for science. When this happens, a [[phase transition]] occurs in the body of knowledge wherein the accepted set of physical norms for scientifically interpreting the laws of a system 'shift' into the new [[paradigm]] of laws, hence Kuhn having coined the term '[[paradigm shift]]'.

##  Impressions
One of the most important books that I've ever read, it helped me think about the natural world in a way that explained a lot of the phenomena I've had a hard time engaging with because they have changed so much over time.

### How I Discovered It
I learned about this book through my college education, specifically during Anthropology courses where Kuhn was used to frame the culture of science.

### Who Should Read It?
Scientists, Anthropologists, and Cultural magnates who want to think about how to change people's minds.

##  How the Book Changed Me
I credit this book with being in part the reason that I think the way that I do today - I think statistically rather than continuously, with the recognition that my thinking and framing can change drastically over time as I'm presented with new information. It introduced me to the [[philosophy of science]], a field of philosophy concerned with better appreciating the changing nature of knowledge and the impact of scientific thinking on how to live one's own life.
