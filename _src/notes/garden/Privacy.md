## Top level summary

If the future is one in which we are connected to everything, we must not let that connection consume us. Being connected does not mean allowing yourself to be seen in entirety, it means choosing what it is that people see of you and keeping the rest sacred. This is the reason that the act of sovereignty is one of allowing oneself to acknowledge that which is completely ours - to release this and allow it to be consumed by any passer-by is to give up our internal sanctified space and allow it to be devalued.

Does this track with the idea that there are cultures where privacy is not a concept shared by the community? I think so, and I think that they exist. In those communities, the concept of privacy is one that acts in opposition to the liminal nature of the whole and what it is up against. Knowing this, it is crucial to understand that the act of privacy is one of preserving a sense of sovereignty not as a fundamental right (because it is not, in times of survival, a full sense of vulnerability is Pareto optimal), but in the face of systems that seek otherwise to consume us. Privacy is an act of self-preservation and rebellion against forces that seek otherwise to make us into their tools - as such we need to ensure that the act of privacy as one of self-preservation is one of establishing a sacred space between ourselves and the systems to which we are a part.

Privacy allows us to modulate the energy that is taken from us so that only what is necessary for a system to exist is consumed - this being the case, privacy is an act of self-_liberation_.
