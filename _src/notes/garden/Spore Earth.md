## How does this fit into the story? How is the world a character?

I think that Spore Earth can be a character through its constant search for a place that the [[Walker]] has to fit into, the different species of the planet constantly interacting and probing its use, molds growing on its skin only not to actually latch on, to flake off because there aren't or weren't any nutrients that they could connect to - so I'm somewhat thinking that it would be interesting to see what happens that has the Walker steadily realize that it doesn't actually belong to the planet it's landed on, seeing the harmony between things as either parasitic or symbiotic, and that it's because of this that there's a beauty to the planet itself, but that the Walker doesn't actually belong to it, it's not a part of this planet that is growing on its own.

The way that the planet could be a character is through the experience the Walker has against treacherous conditions, maybe it's the case that the walkers gets sick and has to upregulate its temperature due to a fungal infection of some sort that gets into its joints and causes there to be slowness in its movement, but it has to do so consciously, or maybe there's a natural upregulation of the heat in its chest that causes the fungus to die off somehow, and this causes the flaking.

Maybe what happens here is that there's a massive fungal storm that happens in the marsh, and everything gets sunken in and coated in a thin film of a pink rain that comes in sideways, slowing everything down. The manner in which this could work is that there could be this film that comes down and as a result there's something that causes everything to slow down, and the effect of this would be that we end one chapter with the inclusion of the storm, of the lack of movement, and then the drying out of this layer is what allows the [[Walker]] to begin moving again, so there's a deep moment of fear where it won't be able to move.
