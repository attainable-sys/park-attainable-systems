## [the Old World is Dying - Jasmine Sun](https://jasmi.news/p/2026-advice)

- "**First, no nostalgia!** The old world was bad. Those staid institutions and rigid hierarchies are not worth mourning."
	- we tend to be nostalgic for a period of time that was actively harmful because we envision that it was 'easier' then, when really the rigidity of what was happening to us was no better than it is now (barring inflation)
	- > "The notion that teens can smoke weed and skim Hegel for four years and have a cushy office job waiting on the other side was always doomed."
	- cognitive work is cheap, physical labor is not
- "**AI makes the world more nepo.** So go make some friends."
	- Yep - something that I already know intuitively. Something I'm trying to do, because I already understand that 'weak ties, strong bonds' is the way to make it all work.
		- “I’m planning to do X, if anyone wants to join me”
	- "You won’t imbibe tacit knowledge in a classroom’s confines, so find a mentor to take you under their wing."
- Keep your identity small, and don't fear starting over - the people who win are those who can remake themselves again and again: to summit one peak, descent it, then hike up the next
	- well, if that's not a description of who I am, I don't know what is.
	- there's no reason you have to be any one thing - the best people aren't. they're just interesting people.
	- 'prestige is a brainworm' meaning that I don't need to chase credentialism
- "Why would anyone hire a 22-year-old with Claude Code when they could just use Claude Code? The AI tools are undifferentiated and everyone has them. Your comparative advantage has to be about _you_."
	- "The reason to write your own essays isn’t that Claude can’t do it better—it’s that writing is thinking and you shouldn’t wither away your brain"
	- using your brain is something you have to choose to do and choose to build on, and it's what the future will demand. that's the difference.
- Struggling is good
	- Sometimes you have to hit your head against a problem for a really really long time in order to experience the reward of having solved it, and *that's good*
## [Clocked Out - Martin Dolan](https://thepointmag.com/criticism/clocked-out/)
- "I wished I was allowed to work hard."
	- This more than anything I think is what people really desire - a sense of purpose in the work they are doing because it causes them to struggle, and struggle is good
	- The fact that everything is getting easier feels like there's a loss of meaning because we were struggling at the things that AI has made easy. All this means is that we have to find new things to struggle with, and that's actually really exciting
		- For instance: have you ever tried to write a short story? it's incredibly hard. try it. it's not easy.
- "At some level, almost everybody wants a good job, one stimulating and rewarding enough to be worth organizing their identity around. The work could, even _should_, be difficult, though ultimately worthwhile."
	- Again, struggle - we want something that tests us, per Primo Levi
- Aspirational work ethic is based on an antiquated understanding of how people succeed:
	- "Free trade would be synonymous with the individual’s right to enterprise, resulting in the “liberal reward” of a flush middle class, the rising tide that raised all ships."

## [How to enter Side Doors](https://velvetnoise.substack.com/p/how-to-enter-side-doors)
- Jobs are search problems
	- it's important to make yourself visible and promote the work you do as part of who you are
		- *I don't really agree with this - because it means that in order to succeed, you can't merely be good at your job; you are also required to tell everyone that you're good at your job somehow. Some people are not able to do that, as much as we might aspire otherwise.*
- "getting a job” [means] finding someone with a problem and convincing them you can solve it."
	- the question becomes asking of oneself what problems one wants to solve rather than 'what do I want to do'
		- I think this makes sense - 'a [[company]] is a group of people trying to make something happen in the world'
	- "They have obvious jobs, which become job posts, and illegible jobs, which hover around the edges as unsolved tension."
- Core to this is asking oneself:
	- What do I care about?
	- Who is doing work I care about?
	- What are they trying to make happen?
	- Where are they stuck?
	- What do I know ow to do, or what could I learn to do, tha would help?
	- Who would be delighted, relieved, or intrigued if I showed up with something useful?
- Side doors create a different kind of signal because it shows you people who are not trying to work through traditional means, and might solve problems in non-traditional ways as well
	- this is how you set yourself apart
	- this is also the reason that the work with [[Prineha Narang - Meeting Prep]] shows me I was willing to solve her problems, and SHE saw that I was the sort of person who knows how to look at problems and understand how *I* might be able to solve them.
	- There are two kinds of side doors:
		- Outbound
			- You put it out into the world that you're interested in working on x style problems by reaching out to the people that you want to work with
			- you sent thoughts to founders / people you want to work with that are insights into what you think are their problems.
				- this is how informational interviews can turn into work. you're getting information about their problems
		- Inbound
			- you put it out into the world that you want to work these problems by SHOWING people that you know how to solve them
- What kinds of 'bounds' to make
	- "An essay can be a small ambassador for your mind."
	- showing people through engagement is a great way to get noticed - it's a way of letting people know that you're paying attention and that you're thinking critically about the problems that they're encountering, then finally that you might be able of solving these problems in novel ways or with novel thinking. it's what sets you apart when there's a need to do so.
