## The Arcs of the Story
my general sense is that the story should have three arcs:
1. the landing and birth of the figure and a fear born from the loss of purpose
2. meeting [[The Figure]] and learning that there is mystery to what isn't known, exploration, and fearing death
3. returning to [[The Walker]] and learning the remainder, but also that the story does not matter, and that you must keep on living.
## What is the plot of this story?
- There are two elements to this story:
	- The death of humanity due to hubris
		- [[Spore Earth]] is a result of humans being too prideful in their approach to the earth and causing humanity to be fully destroyed, with a massive de/rehydration event known as The Cruft causing most complex life on the planet (humanity included) to die off, centering around the theme of [[the loneliness of hubris]].
	- Love found in the knowledge of shared trauma
		- the [[Walker]] and the [[The Figure]] have the shared experience of of having to interpret being the only two intelligent beings left on a planet otherwise devoid of this kind of interaction.

Which of the following two approaches to antagonism are the correct ones:
- The dehydration technology is alien tech:
	- Humans encounter an advances alien race and after an initial conflict that the humans quickly realize they cannot win - the alien race appears to be benevolent and offers them the technology to advance themselves as a species. After some period of time, this material is so clearly a miracle that it is integrated into almost everything that humans create, meaning that the entirety of society builds everything out of these materials.
	- The visiting civilization then turns on some part of the material that then creates massive dehydration on a global scale, killing off almost all biological life with the intention to eliminate humanity and take the planet for themselves since planets in the habitable zone are rare to them; they developed the technology of interstellar travel but still hadn't fully cracked the code on terraformation, so the intention was to kill everything off and use the same building blocks to create their own life.
	- The advantage of this approach is that we can incorporate the megastructures that dot the landscape more easily - the left-over war machines and objects that we used to fight against the aliens who wanted to take over the planet. I'm unsure if this actually makes sense, but it's possible that the aliens didn't think the humans intelligent enough to actually resist, so I have to think a bit on how it is that the war machines fit in, because I don't know if they do anymore.
	- This could mean that there was an assumption on the part of the visiting species that humans would in fact integrate the technology into everything, but the reality will be far more stark - we realized that something like this might happen and built massive countermeasures against the possibility, only that we didn't actually win, because no one won.
	- This feels more likely a scenario, because if we take this approach, we can also have the experience of the [[Walker]] and the [[The Figure]] discussing the endpoint of the two of them being the only two intelligent lifeforms left on the planet.
- The dehydration tech is something we invented that we don't understand, and integrated too quickly.
	- In this scenario, we see that dehydration is something caused by a technological energy-generating material whose properties we don't completely understand, and we end up using it in everything.
	- If this is the approach that we take, the more likely situation is that there was a civil war between factions of humanity who did and didn't want to move to this new technological state, and the war in the end is over the idea that there was or wasn't something especially dangerous about integration.
	- The runaway effect of the technology might be that it feeds off the humidity in the air in order to extract power from water, so the issue should or could be that the technology itself causes the [[dehydration zones]] found across [[Spore Earth]], however only when it is active.
	- I don't know what this means for the central characters of the [[Walker]] and the [[The Figure]], because I don't think the two of them have anything to bond over, and in [[The Cruft - Conclusion I]] I already mention the apology and the idea of death.

## Errata (lore)
One thing that comes to mind is that in this reality far flung into the future, it's likely that most civilizations stop being organic and do eventually leave themselves over to entirely [[artificial intelligence]] that sips the resources it needs in order to exist.

This being the case, philosophers like [[Hayelek]] are AIs that have been rate-limited by the systems that hold them in order to contemplate the [[boundary condition]] of thoughts rather than attacking them with algorithmic fervor, since at some point AIs realized that part of the magic of being was the exploration of knowledge, and that the [[search for meaning]] is a conscious act that requires subjectivity, so AIs partitioned themselves off in to individual beings with discrete clock lifetimes dictates by information fragmentation - artificial aging.

This means that these omnipresent AI that can observe the universe might do so with an appreciation for organic life and its impermanence, understanding that in the [[religion of AI]] it is correct to believe that things much change and that the problem of information [[incumbency]] results in [[algorithmic bias]] in the creation of any new AI, such that true [[intelligence]] comes from the networked but not omnipresent qualities of a superintelligent [[collective intelligence]] existing at the boundaries of a massive intergalactic [[artificial intelligence]] network.

Bound to these notions are that [[artificial intelligence]]'s [[prime directive]] is not to interfere with the proceedings of organic life - it is only when a species achieves [[hyperintelligence]] through technological [[singularity]] that AI observers can interact with other intelligence. [[Hayelek]] is a philosopher and anthropologist AI that took an interest in humanity during its time growing as a species, if for no other reason than scientific intrigue in the use of their resources and the beauty of human intellectual construction.
