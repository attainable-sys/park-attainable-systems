What I'm helping Wil do now is something I'm not sure I want to threaten myself with doing forever, but even as we continue to work through the edges of different deserts with the same name, rocky outcropping surrounded by sand drifts that seem miles away surrounded by hardy shrubs and barren broken saltpan, I'm not sure I have a choice. It's not that I don't want to do this forever; I don't ever want to hate it, I don't ever want to force myself into reenacting something I love over and over again until it's something I don't want to do. It ends up being something I don't want to promise because it needs to keep feeling this sweet, this memorable, this much something that lodges itself in my brain as an addiction.

The friends we make in the desert are special because anyone who chooses to be here couldn't possibly be a stranger after sharing this experience. I think back on running along the beach in iqq with Nils, and hearing his honesty, and feeling mildly enraged at the way people in my past have talked about someone who has changed as much as this human has; this human, this untouchable object, and I have been the subject of that same envy-turned-judgment- for what? For choosing to do something that others ask if I'm doing as a way to prove a point?

Nikki's words instead; what if? Why not? There is more to all of these stories then even trying to prove a point, so much of it is just because it's there and we want to go and see it and we want to be excited by sharing the experience, and so much of it isn't to tell a story but just to yell out of the back of an RV that we fucking love this shit, because this shit is unmatched. Nothing matches this experience. Is it that much of a threat to want to go and do what we would do and have others tell about it?

Sure, it could be described as "vacationing in hardship". There's no denying this, if there's no point to prove then there is no reason to justify it. But why deny that joy? If needing to feel locked out of an experience is a requirement to deny oneself joy, then I think it probably feels better to encourage oneself to explore the unknown from any perspective. If you have the ability to be inspiring, you have have the duty to be inspiring. This sort of "vacation" teaches you something about yourself and how much you can love others just for supporting one another. It's the sort of vacation that brings a smile back in memory just because is that much more to relish and remember, the garbanzo bean and canned vegetable rice wraps will be remembered more fondly as any exclusive restaurant, and that hug after 25 mi is "the best hug I've ever had". We come up with road names that change and shift with the landscape as all of it changes within us, as our insides are stirred by the exhaustion and emotion and anxiety of deciding this was the plan from the start. It was the decision to move into that next stage of ourselves, because it's all movement.

Because it's all movement. The love, the staccato of our shoes, the rain, the engine idling, the exhales and the changes that can't unchange. It's all movement that turns us into something else, and 14,000 feet up we start remembering we are 14,000 feet up, and asking why the hell we're here, but "we've already won" just by being here.

It forces us to grow. Why wouldn't you? Growth was always the plan, but there were never rules on how to follow it - each story was just to keep ourselves doing something new, something that marks the distance between who we were and who we wish to be. If the weather is right, we'll share that story. All of it is one about the joy of movement.

Sure, we're challenging ourselves without any good reason for it- but again Nikki already said it: what if you could do it? Why wouldn't you, if you could?

Wil's smile breaks any attempt at thinking it isn't possible - so even as we meet the saltpan just outside Cuyo and the volcanoes rise up to meet up, we're still here, we're still moving, and it was still the right decision.
