[[Children]] at times are freeform angels - people unsculpted by expectation, whose frames flop and sinter about in the adoption of movement. I watch a woman coax her child down the sideward; it takes six and a half minutes of ill-fitting communication, the child expressing without words an expectation their mother seeks with which to compete - the child bare-chested under their rebellion as the sweater they wear rides up and away from the mother's grasp, away from the home their mother wants to be enticing. Maybe before children learn words they know better than we do how to be resolute in the expression of their desires. It's more pure a declaration of want than when working in dialogue with a fear of judgment of conflict. I think parents forget this; it is easy to forget.
My mother, raising three children somewhere she didn't speak the language just because her husband wanted to chase his career. She wanted to leave as well, according to him. "It was the fastest way out of the United States," in one version of the truth, "we were only going to be there for three years, not twenty," in another. We lived in nine countries. My mother raised us in all of them. My father was there, sometimes, most of the time, and years later before he knew I was a woman he would tell me what he would do when he wasn't.
Thirty six years later, it has shaped me not to feel American because of it, to look at the country on my passport with skepticism. My parents love one another more than they ever have, in a sense I don't care about what brought them to this point, even while I bear the marks of how it's shaped me. We are all shaped by people who did not know how to raise children. Maybe that is why I am impassive in my love for my parents; they made mistakes, and I will as well, even when the child I raise will not come from my distended belly: I will not know what to do with a child either. I will not know how to speak their non-language of unawareness of a need to express desire. I will also fuck up. I will still love them. They'll still hate me through my mistakes, until they are old enough to understand those mistakes were impossible to avoid. I will also pull a reluctant child down the sidewalk and not listen. I cannot wait. I cannot wait for the frustration of not knowing how to translate the tense wind of a child's voice expressing opposition. The purring wilderness of wanting something different.
