It was about ten feet long, mottled grey, and had the same texture as granite. I think if I hadn't known about stone already, I'd have uncovered it and wouldn't have known that it didn't belong there, but a 4-year degree in geology leading to a masters in schist incursions told me otherwise. Besides, it didn't really _feel_ like granite. There was something about it that felt at once less dense, but far heavier - as though it had the weight of an object, not a part of the landscape.

There was also something about it that told me I shouldn't tell anyone I'd found it. On the surface of it, sure, it was probably a little weird that a geology professor from a local college was spending their weekends digging pilot cores in the a neighborhood state park - but something told me that it was more than this. It wasn't until I uncovered it that it really clicked - I'd spent a week carefully peeling back the layers to reveal whatever had broken the tip of my spiral bore - but this realization had me settled in knowing that whatever this was was more than just a curiosity.

When I first figured out its total size, I didn't know what to do. I didn't have the skills to understand what to do next, so I took a ridiculous number of photographs. Somehow I was motivated to take these on film rather than digitally, as though taking pains to make sure that there wasn't a trace that I'd found this thing in the woods. This was echoed by how I covered the object back up, building a lattice of branches and covering it in dead leaves. I wasn't too worried someone would stumble on what I'd found, it was in a strange spot and at best, maybe a dog would run over to the area and piss on it.

When I developed the photos in my darkroom, things started to feel a bit more ominous. The images showed something completely different from what I'd photographed. In place of quiet-looking stone, there was a sleek, ornate, organic shape that approximated a minimal surface egg, a mathematical object that I'd remembered from the Topology courses I took alongside my master's program. It was a gunmetal gray, swiss-cheesed with paths through the object, and there was no way that this had developed naturally - the filigree-like pathways through the object made it appear symmetrical, all the more organic. There was no way this was a rock.

Also, the photographs had the object standing up.

When the first photos developed, this was the part that struck me with the most dissonance. The photographs looked as though I'd taken them of the object while it was standing on this end, several steps back in order to capture the entirety of the shape in frame. Worse yet, I couldn't shake the feeling that it was looking at me through the image - the same feeling a person has when they look back on a portrait of a friend looking directly into the frame - but I couldn't discern where the eyes might be. The whole thing seemed as though it was staring into the lens.

It was about 8pm when I developed the first images - just the time that the state park closed. After I'd gotten over the chill feeling of not knowing that sat in the forefront of my chest, so similar to a feeling of devastation, I packed a flashlight, a strobe, and my jacket, hopping back into my car to drive back toward the object. I ended up having to park in the middle school parking lot, toward the back, hoping that the car seemed inconspicuous, knowing that it probably wasn't - but when I drove into the lot there were no police patrolling the area, so I tried my luck. I was lucky that my training had me noting down the lat/long coordinates of my pet neighborhood incursion studies, having logged the location of the dig into a fitness watch, but I hadn't taken this approach to the site, and it was dark, and it was late November. There would be some bushwhacking involved.

I walked about a hundred feet into the woods guided by the watch, careful to let my eyes adjust, before I flipped on my flashlight in bug-proof mode and bathed the forest in front of me in red light. It clicked for me that I was doing something unusual - but the power that intrigue had over me was substantial. I was compelled, methodically stepping over the macabre shadows of logs and pushing aside bushes and branches, following the dim arrow on the LED surface of my watch with its proximity decreasing.

I watched the distance tick to zero, my mind finally growing accustomed to a recognition of my surroundings in a different light, and sure enough - the lattice of sticks and leaves were still over laying across my dig site. I reached into the loam I'd piled up in a corner of the site to pull out a rake I'd brought with me several digs before and began to clear away the cover I'd built. The object definitely wasn't standing. I took the photographs out of the backpack I'd brought with me, flipping the flashlight to natural glow, and again, the image of the object standing erect in the woods, light passing through the gaps, stared back at me from the photograph.

I decided to risk taking more photographs. I realized before I thought about doing so that I'd fully uncovered the object again, and before pulling out my camera, I shone my flashlight over the surface of the object. It looked no different than it had before, a granite-like texture that I couldn't place.

I set up my strobe - stepping back from the pit I'd dug up and positioned myself to take the image. Nothing was different. I looked around the woods, hoping nothing was looking back at me, but curiosity and a little bit of fear goaded me onward. It was not probable anyone would see the strobe go off unless they were in the woods with me. That was a chance I'd have to take.

I armed the strobe and the remote transmitter, stepped back, setting my focus at the distance I knew I was from the object, and depressed the shutter. One technique that I'd learned from shooting was to keep both eyes open when aiming, so that an object's framing in the final image matches what you see. The strobes went off.

For a split second, I knew I saw something other than what I'd been aiming at. The strobed image on my retina was of the object, on its end, lit against the night sky, long, tendrilled shadows passing through the objects holes. It wasn't what I'd come into the woods to find. _Fuck. There was no way that just happened._

I stepped over to the dig pit, and the object clearly hadn't moved. Fear started creeping into my chest, but I stepped back to take another photograph. It didn't make any sense. I was aiming downward, but the image on my retina was looking upward, at the object against the canopy; not the image I was about to take again.

I held my breath, finger slowly pushing down the shutter. The strobe fired, and again, stark against my retinas was the object, staring back at me from a frame of the canopy against stars. _There's no way this is happening._

I didn't know what to do. I lowered the camera and looked at the object, still lying in situ. It hadn't moved, there was no way that it had moved. I hesitated, breath tight in my chest. Looked down at my watch.

It was 3am. I'd been in the woods for 9 hours. _There's no way this is happening._

I packed up my things. Something told me to cover the object back up, but I was terrified. Something was happening, here, something I didn't have words for, and that I knew no one would understand if I explained to them - it could only be if I showed them. When the object was covered back up, I stood back, looking at my dig site again. _Fuck_. I turned and began walking back, and immediately realized I hadn't logged the location of the parking lot or my track through the woods. _FUCK._

_Calm down._ I pulled out my phone, pulled open my maps application. I wasn't far, I just had to head in the general direction of the lot. Thank goodness for technology. _Fuck._

40 minutes slogged by. I didn't recognize anything I'd walked over getting to the site. Nothing looks the same in reverse. I kept checking my phone, every twenty feet or so, inching closer to the lot. _Fuck._ Terror was building in me. _What the fuck did I just find?_

At the edge of the lot, I saw my car, cloistered toward the edge of the lot. No police in the lot. Good. I walked toward my car, opened up the trunk, put down my things, opened the driver's side door, sat down. _What the fuck did I just find?_ Turned on the car engine. _What the fuck did I just find?_
