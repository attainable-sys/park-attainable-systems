I have to remember what I have learned and why it is important to read theory and fiction.

"You should read more" comes from a place of deep misunderstanding about the generalized unrest people it is not a question of a life lived in the context of new ideas, but rather the people can or should be learning constantly. It's my loose contention that learning constantly provides us with the tools to deal with the problems with me encounter, but I am deeply cognizant of the teleological gap that arises in not having one's own needs, met at a basic level and the encouragement to educate oneself. After all, we cannot fill stomachs with words.

This is maybe the place from which to make the most significant inquiry of what uses education if the utility of this education cannot be implemented due to systemic barriers placed in the path of resilience the pen is only mightier than the sword given the paper on which to communicate ideas without the means to transmit into action, the philosophy, we hope to inspire people with violence seems well positioned to take the shape of action little inspired by anything other than a desire to cease or ease one's suffering.

 It is often addressed in films this way how do the well-intentioned deviate from the morality when pinned like a fly to the Styrofoam backboard of a world so feebly built to support us? Morality is in this case situation--we can only build better worlds if we have the means to.

 But we do continue to fight. We remember that those who have fought longest and hardest are those who continue to do so when every other person ceases to this is because it is the case that people who have never felt safe, will forever stay the course of seeking such safety.--and it is my contention that apathy(including my own) likely comes from a place of significant privilege.

That is--I know I will be fine and this is the reason I have the security to feel ease: I can only do so because I can consider the absurdity of having to make choices to keep myself alive, further under emphasizing that I experienced an increased degree of general attendance in the circumstance of any threat to my well-being. I will in almost every frame of reference (save one glaring circumstance, obvious to those who know me well) be fine, probably. This is the reason I can sit back on my heels and feel frustrated not with whether or not I have the means to survive, but instead how I specifically would like to survive.

Meaningfully it very much, could be the case that the reason I possessed the self-awareness to acknowledge this truth is due to the singular and unique circumstance of my own personal oppression--maybe I would otherwise be completely ignorant to my security if I didn't have to face one particular imposing fear upon my existence that call into question the security otherwise experience. I don't know because I only know my own experience to start with.

However, this last observation is important and serves to represent the final tail-eating point of what I write here. I can only speak to my own experience and it's commonality with that of others.--but I could not conceive of the relatability of my experience to parallels or lack there of without seeking out that same parallelism. It is critically this seeking out that impart to the act of reading and learning about both the real and fictional experiences of others it's critically importance. I may not be conscious of it, but every time I imagine the existence of the "other", I glean some small understanding of the parallax between our identities, and my hope is that with each of the perambulations into the possibility space existing between experiences, I am opening up just a bit more to the possibility within me to engage in a sympathetic residence with them I do not know if this is true, but it is what I like to believe. A combination of qualitative appreciation between the differences of shared realities, and most importantly, the sheer obligation to utilize my imaginative skill, requires me an obligation to as well grow as a person.

This, if among less weight and morally oriented, justifications, is why I read. I read to better understand myself, and to understand other others, and it works, more often than not. Better yet, I think (I think) it gives me hope.
