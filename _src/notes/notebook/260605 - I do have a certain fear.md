I do have a certain fear for men. I think broadly the attitudes of [[Adrienne Rich]] and [[Sontag]] and all of those we look up to with so much zeal was the right one - that we as women do not have the responsibility to care for men. I agree with this. When the male gender has been in a position of control and power for so long then are caused to lose this power at which point the entire gender flails in public in some display of disaparagement and self-pity - we as women, again, are not responsible for this flailing. We do not need to coddle them. Yet I still fear for them: I do find myself hoping that if we have *abandoned* them in the ways we have chosen to do in the last fifty years since the turning of feminism, we must pay at least some attention to what they have done in response to this abandonment. Not as a question of responsibility, but rather as a responsive *measure*, if nothing else.
The reason for this is that I don't think it is right to think that we can utterly abandon the social fabric that has shaped modern society (read: I am writing this from the West, so I can only speak to how it has shaped the West), we do in some senses have a responsibility to steel ourselves and our children to the impact of men not knowing what to do with the assertion of will by women over society.
Read: we have made tremendous progress, as women. What I mean by this is that today, we are debating not whether or not women are equal (we are not, there is still work to be done) but whether or not some women are 'women enough' to be included in the canon of womanhood. What a privilege, comparative to the progress that has been made, and to our general standing in the annals of time, across our societies. Not only can women vote, but we have earned many more needful freedoms, and we have far more freedom to orient toward so many other forms of success. It is magnificent!
Women are solidly represented across the workforce (if unequally in terms of profession, but as I said, there is work to be done here). However [we do not yet earn more than our 'counterparts'](https://www.forbes.com/sites/hollycorbett/2026/03/26/equal-pay-day-2026-the-bonus-gap-and-more-wage-gap--contributors/), despite the rumors that some think we do. Presently, the work to be done still has women overrepresented in low-wage jobs, which skews the data southward in terms of total money time women have to work in order to make as much as their male counterparts, with the glaring impact of bonuses making up for the largest disequaling for closing the gap - men are rewarded for their work at a level that women have few ways of fighting for. Despite this the 'controlled gap' (measured as an averaging of women/men rated against equal qualifications and jobs) [brings the gap much closer](https://www.payscale.com/featured-content/gender-pay-gap), which is great! However the former gap is usually described as the 'opportunity gap', reflecting how much harder it is, generally, for women to seek these same opportunities available to men.
That is to say, women are in a better position than ever before, but there is still much to work on - and it is not the purpose of this essay.
What I seek to write about instead is the importance of paying attention to what then men 'do' when they are abandoned. I fear for the fact that when men are 'abandoned' they do not know what to do, chiefly when a part of a society that has structured itself around the servile role of women in relation to men. this is why men are pining for a previous era.
There is a book that I think of often; the book is the origin of the phrase 'paradigm shift'. Thomas Kuhn wrote this book in the 7-'s, in the wake of a scientific reimadining of reality iunder the advent of relativity and quantum technologies. Whet he discerned was that when a society is fiven enough information to change its mind, it will resist the urge to do so until the fate of the change is inevitable. I think we are in this time.
I think we are in this time because as e watch men capriciously try toremake the world into what they had wanted it to be, they are doing so in response to what they most fear: the loss of their power. They fear the possibility that they might have to be responsible for themselves, and they fear that the order that was established which alloed thm not to have to make that effort is an event that they will have to respond to - and so they have handily rejected it, trying to bring in the previous order.
The really cool thing about Kuhn is that he believes paradigm shifts are inevitable, no matter how much rejection the system tries to push upon the opposition to the norm. He believes that when there is a systemic rejection to a sort of science by the zeitgeist, this is an inoculating gesture before the system is forced to assimilate in the new norm and progress forward.
Because of this, I am optimistic, but I am still concerned. I am concerned because I don't want men to flail. I am seeing certain kinds of men appear that people are calling 'performative' and my immediate reaction is to exclaim 'even when they are trying, they are wrong? what do you want from them?' I see them and they give me hope because performance is all we have to cause change. At some point pretension gives way to the performance becoming the norm. In his work 'Pretentiousness: Why It Matters', Dan Fox makes a compelling argument that outlines exactly this requirement - it is important if not an obligation of every person to perform what it is that they expect to be. Why should men not 'perform' in this way? The performance gives way to actually acting the right way, if even there is a period of time where this performance does not take hold.
I am optimistic because watching the power system flail feels just as it should. It has collateral damage, as with any flailing. However, I do think the flailing is an indication. What I am worried about is that the men who don't follow the change find themselves entrenched and depressed because they are depressed, and this is the more terrifying component of it all. Angry men are violent in their desire to have the right to be violent, but it doesn't mean that they will win.
