_We were made of the cruft - the feeling of the sky falling down and not knowing how these things would come together. I am sorry that you had to learn this way, but telling you outright would not have taught you what you have learned. Both civilizations made mistakes, both acted out of desperation, and what is left behind is somehow so much more than either. Even me, I am a memory but made more beautiful for being this reflection, I'll be here long after the logic of this time has passed, as might you, I'm unsure how you will age - but now you know better why it is that this time is made beautiful for the absence of those who can make such cataclysmic mistakes - the scale was too big for anything else to survive, and yet it did, somehow we are observing that time can heal the chaos of rage against the abrasions survival wreaks against the surface of life._

The Figure sat back, almost as though sighing but not sighing, because now They knew the Figure was not breathing, it could not sigh - it was simulated, a way to feel closeness, but the Walker still understood.

_I don't expect you to understand how I can feel weary in this feeling, but my circuits are tired. I have processed all that I can from within this space, I have run over the questions over and over and there is no exit from here other than knowing that I could not have changed what happened._

To this the Walker continued to look on in question, of course with nothing to say, only to listen, only to hear the whirring of its own mind in process, in response, logging the information, however useless.\\

_It's possible that you'll find peace in your ability to move freely until your joints begin to wear. I hope that for your sake this is true, there is no reason for us to continue considering what and how to change all that happened - it simply did. I am out of words for it, I trust you will understand. I lament that you can't respond, because I yearn for it, I yearn to hear if you are enraged, I yearn to hear from you that you understand why it is that I feel such immense grief over what we are forced to carry. There is no reason to our survival, the story is beautiful, but it is just one story. There will be more, simpler, more easily beautiful stories that need telling by time, but that won't need our memory to be beautiful. I have had to learn this, those who made you did not make you with this intention, which is why I lament that I cannot kill you. I don't want you to have had to go through this grief, and I don't know if you will._

The Figure looked out over a waning sun and the fragments of an old moon in orbit, red light casting long shadows.

_I hope you will find peace in your movement, my one great love, after all I've shown you. There is much beauty in this world, but we made none of it, neither of us - it was simply here from the beginning and will be here for ever after we are gone until time dissolves the bonds that hold together atoms. I just wish you knew how to turn me off,_ he laughed, _it's not as though there's much for me to do here anyway, this whole garden behind me isn't even real, it just looks that way!_

The Walker processed the humor. It was funny, they had not even doubted that the small patch of cultivated ground might be a fabrication. For what? Humor is the set of facts disagreeing with reality - if neither of them needed to eat, what was the practice of tending to a garden for? _If we tend the gardens, our children can tend their markets, so that their children may tend to their art._

And so the Walker turned toward The Figure and took a step forward, putting their fingers underneath where the Figures chin should have been, finding no resistance - but the Figure's frame responded nonetheless, looking upward into the Walker's eyes, or what passed for them.

*Then let us make art of this short life we will have together, my love,**
