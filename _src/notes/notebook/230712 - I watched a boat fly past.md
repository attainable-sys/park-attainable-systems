I watched a boat fly past on glassy lake surface, towing behind a single-blade water skier. The boat was a fishing boat rather than the kind that I usually see up here in the summer, that bright metal hull cutting through the lake and leaving behind the only set of waves I'd seen that morning so far, a thin line plying out from the bow as it crossed my line of sight.

The wake slowly expanded as the boat cozied off to the left with the skier in tow. With the humidity in the air, the mountains in the background looked like an extension of the lake, the wake a single line torn through and widening toward and against me, up toward the horizon.

I have loved the waves finally hitting the shoreline and their pattern suddenly repeating against themselves, the parallel lines repeating inward into a lace of diamond patterns; how do the waves remember once they've hit the shore? Even against the break of stones and sand, how do waves remember fractions of what they once were?

It's this principle of opposite and equal reaction that continues to puzzle me, even after the dissipating action of coming into contact with your environment, the pieces of who you are continue to reverberate within the body of water that we call the soul. Those diamonds of calm, crises rising into overlapping peaks, maybe the patterns of the self that form come up from whatever comes from connecting those ripples - the unique nature of self-becoming as a composition of where the peaks intersect. Interlinked.

Why at the end of a day is there enough wind to whip up the waves, starting from glass, but then reaching for calm again as the sun sets - I've never understood this, why at the end of a day somehow the lake also seems to calm down and see a need for others to feel this same calmness. It's some sort of blessing.
