I find that I often begin aging myself in advance of my next birthday by about four months. It could be that this is a function of aging, and I simply don't care anymore; the notion of needing celebrate a given day feels less important when in the wake of accumulated years this single day feels like one among many.
Yes this year, I celebrated by birthday with complete strangers. This is one of those behaviors I have earned and kept up with, not exclusive to birthdays, but to other celebrations I've had in life. I first did this by celebrating Thanksgiving with a Christian musician in Georgia in a field at night with two guitars and a plastic tub of chicken tenders we'd bought at Kroger. I celebrated Christmas with a thatch of drunk Chileans, Argentineans, English, French, and Germans on a beach in Ecuador after feasting on wild-caught fish in a local's backyard. The bonfire we build was so high that it seemed to lick the fabric of the night sky with its flames, and in the search for more downed wood my friend (she is still my friend) sat me down and told me to enjoy the night that we'd made. I spent of of those Easters cycling across Japan weith trhee men, and the day of may have been when I had to remove a tick from the skin of one friend's scrotum with a pair of tweezers while laying in a room full of other traveling strangers, having carried our bikes up over a thousand steps to get to where we lay. Another Thanksgiving was during an ice storm in Kansas, where trucks littered the highway median jack-knifed, and I ate a Sizzler at a Denny's where once we left, the rusting towbar of my truck came loose, throwing sparks across the highway, and I pulled over to swing the last bit until it broke off completely, and we slept at a rest stop that played a farmer's weather radio station on a muffled speaker all night until we woke up covered in snow.
It's not that I don't like celebrating occasions, it's that I'd rather use their marker as a way to remember something else. My sense is that a meal that acts the same as the same meal from the year before will be remembered that way. I won't forget my 36th birthday. I ran along Col de Fours with three men in a race and whooped as loud as I could with my lungs full of mountain air, and was chastised by a Frenchman stranger who said they don't do that here. I felt like an ignorant American despite my native roots, when we left that man behind, my new Bavarian friend told me that they did the same where he came from, so it was okay. People have different ways of responding to the mountains. I took a photo when we reached the pass with the three of them, and Benni sent me a message afterward, asking forgiveness that he'd placed it among his other memories as a way to mark the occasion. By nightfall that same day, the route I'd made had sent an Italian and an Englishman over a pass between France and Italy into Bardonecchia as the sun went down, and they said it was the scariest, hardest thing they'd ever done, but that they'd do it again in a heartbeat. My birthday ended with a shot from a bottle of Genepy on a folding table next to a brown Renault van at the top of Col de Vars, and the sad, smiling puppy of a Frenchman who'd marked the checkpoints of our race got me a sparkling pair of socks with 'Badass' written on the side as a gift.
There are better systems for making memories than those we're told to use. The unimportant things, I'm not as good at remembering, and as I get older I care less about trying to. I make stories from the things that *are* important, instead. They aid in granting gravity to the experiences with other than I want to share, and for months afterward, ticking into years, those memories work hard not to be filed away. The folders I could tuck them inside feel golden, and on the sorting desk of my mind I can't help but keep them there for a git longer. A bit of suffering, together. A laugh. The dark. Caipirinha or champagne on a beach on a sullen beach, with stranger. Frigate bird wings, ripping through the air like torn paper. Life is richer that way.
What I am saying is this: there are systems for memory, and there are good memories. We can make the choice to remembering things, however inane, or we can choose to make memories we will crave never to put away. Think of this. Fo the next time you celebrate the turning of a year, don't try and repeat what you have already done. Ask instead: what is the strangest, most brilliant thing I can do to ensure that it is worth remembering?
