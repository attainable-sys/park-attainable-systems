She piped up from the back of the room, standing up.

"Well - professor, if you'll let me say it - I think all of us agree that none of us knew we'd end up here. I don't think it's fair for us to talk about our current conditions as though there was something we could have done to prevent it. None of us knew that what we were doing would have these results."

I cocked a slight smile, keeping my eyes locked in her direction.

"Continue?"

"So like - We act as though we should all feel bad for how things turned out, but I'm just not really convinced that it was something we could have done anything about. Everything was proceeding to this point, and now we're here. There's not a lot we can do to walk back the idea of changing where we are. It feels like the best thing we can do is start from where we are."

Another voice spoke up, a boy sitting in the third row. Rimmed glasses.

"It's not really about us atoning for what we did, I don't think that's what the professor is trying to have us understand. We need to know how we got to where we are so that we can work to dismantle the structures that are resulting in the present. We can't really change things right now unless we know how we got here."

She hesitated, looked back at me.

"Do you want to respond to that?"

"Yeah - like, I get the idea there, and I'm not disagreeing with it at all. I think we definitely need to know what brought us here, but look at what we learned this semester. A lot of things are random, some of the reasons that things are happening have more to do with the interaction of two or three events that had unpredictable outcomes. I'm not just convinced that we can find the answer in looking at the past."

There was some silence.

"Why might that be?"

"I - well, I think that there's value in it, but the random nature of our time puts us in the condition of trying to explain a present that is currently unfolding. My impression is that by trying to decipher too much of what it took for right now to happen might result in a constant state of reflection rather than one of action. I think if we're instead looking at the present and deciding what needs to happen next, then maybe we can get ahead of things getting any worse."

The boy in the rimmed glasses responded again.

"We can't look at the present without understanding how the present came to be the present,"

"And I'm not arguing with that, I know there's a degree of that, I just - I don't think that it's what we should be obsessed with. There's too much at stake for us to want to get through and too many dangers of us continuing on with the way things are right now."

"An obsession with how the past unfolded is what all science is based on, though. You can't solve the present without having done analysis of the past. The idea that you can work from the present without having to know that seems short-sighted."

She faltered a bit. The boy with the rimmed glasses sat back down, looking back at me.

"Our current condition is a result of the actions we took in our past. This is true. I think Mr Sales is correct to bring up that some degree of analysis is incontrovertibly important to the process of examining any path forward from a disposition. Looking into the past and tabulating the work that took us toward certain ill-conceived outcomes is crucial,"

"However, while I think you're both right in your own ways, but what Ms Reedin just said is more correct than you are, Mr Sales. What she is right to point out is in opposition to your core position. We cannot recreate the past, therefore we cannot hope to create the future from a knowledge of the past."

I could see Sales squirm in his seat, hoping to respond.

"This does not mean that we don't need to know the past, it means that we are obliged to understand that correcting course on the issues that are ahead of us requires new ways of thinking that are more resourceful than the ones we have employed so far,"

"Resourcefulness requires courage, and courage is illogical. It's possible to make measured guesses at what you think will happen in most situations, but some degree of faith is necessary in order to get toward the solutions we hope to see unfold. There is no certainty. That is what I've hoped to teach you all in this course, which is why Ms Reedin's comment on present disposition is where I want us to start from. What are the basics? What are we starting from, and what tools do we have at our disposal?"

Sales blurted out

"But our tools are the past!"

"This is true, some of the past can be used as a tool. However, you are committing the sin of overfitting - you are believing that past trends can somehow predict future outcomes. Any scientist worth their salt will tell you this is not true, and that the results themselves _as they unfold_ are more truthful than a study of the past. Ms Reedin's comment has us understand that we need to consider the variety of optionality that the present offers us, because there is potential in understanding that a path forward _from the present_ can be informed by any approach, meaning that it's possible for us to completely change our approach in opposition to any and all past proceeding. It's very likely that a resolution to our current condition lies in taking the problem from a completely different angle, approaching it with some other tool than prediction. Sometimes, if you want to knock something off course, you need to blow something else up, and this, more than anything else, lies in doing the unexpected."
