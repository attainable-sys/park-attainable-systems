After several years of covering running, I began running in 2019.

In the years leading to the present that love has fallen apart and come back together several times, as many religions do. My faith in movement has been shaken, dusted off, and rooted more firmly by the miles that sew it together.

These have been the years where running has migrated from intrigue, to mental health tool, to injury, to fatigue, to an imperative and finally into a form of freedom. This transition had to do with the pandemic and with changing states of self, but it also had to do with realizing there was something more in the staccato of other people's footfalls that I'd been chasing beyond merely getting to know other athletes - a sort of transcendental state that I'd grown used to watching people fall into. I started chasing that state in myself.

When I say 'covering running,' I'm describing the five preceding years to 2019 following running subculture, mostly by bicycle with cameras in the basket, primarily following some of the more critically unhinged in the community - people who throw themselves into the moving traffic of a moving city, people who run hundreds of miles across humid states in the heat of summer. People whose entire lives (at least for the period of time that I had come to know them) were about how to get out over the next stretch of miles.

I have made lasting friendships in this coverage and many of these people have ended up with their accolades; first woman and second place overall in the Last Annual Vol State 500k (thank you @rhodasmoker), a trice of top ten women marathon (I see you @jmackattack, @caitlinphillips, @wicked.la), several magazine covers (hello @iamlshauntay, @samuel4nderson), inspirations that echo in the people they meet (love and more love for @matthewlukemeyer) and finally, those who had brought me to the very front of it all from the beginning (unending appreciation for @orchardstreetrunners for this).

But when I really started running, the people who showed me why I should run are people I still run with, and are the people with whom I will run to the ends of the earth - the sort of people whose skin is dusty with the slow movement of the sun across countless days, and those who chase its progress beyond the horizon under a blanket of stars until it returns the next morning. These are friends with whom I have covered countless thousands of miles. People with whom I have found religion. People who are willing to take on the steel rush of semi trucks on empty tarmac in a hundred deserts. People I can't tag, for a variety of reasons, but who all of you know.

This does not toss aside those who showed me that there is something here to chase; I would not have gotten here without them--but it does reveal the cornucopic spectra of what people find in hammering out distance. Some of us want the speed. Some of us want the distance. All of us want the free movement of the air in our lungs, and those of us who want the deserts keep finding one another out there in its emptiness, over and over.

This is what has brought me here, at least. A religion of sorts, cobbled together with shoelaces, maltodextrin, laughter, and caloric deficits. A religion I'll keep practicing because it's something holier than needing to explain it away, a religion I keep close to me because a relationship with this sort of faith in oneself doesn't require explaining to others, because those who understand already understand. If you know, you really do know.

So I'll see all of you out there sometime soon
