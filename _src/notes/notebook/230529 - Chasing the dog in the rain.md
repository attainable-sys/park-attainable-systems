What was one of your proudest moments?

Oddly enough, I think one of my proudest moments was the time that I chased after the family dog in the rain. At the time, I was in college, and I had just driven back from my college town to visit my parents. The drive was long, something like 9 hours, and I knew I was getting home to my parents not being there. It was late in the afternoon, and I got there in the middle of a rainstorm, pulling my car up to the garage so that I could transfer the belongings I had with me into the house without all of them getting too wet. I opened up the garage door with a couple of different bags in my arms, and went up to the internal door within the garage, unlocking it and seeing my parents dog there, wagging her tail at me after not having seen me in several months. I walked through the threshold and into the kitchen, moving to put down my bags, and taking off my shoes in the process - and when I had turned around to the open door, I could see the dog out on the front lawn, scampering off in the direction of the busy highway right by my house. Still barefoot, I took off after her, calling her name, and that's when the rain really started to come down. As many husky owners know, chasing after a husky that has gotten loose can be a losing battle, because they usually see it as play and will take off even faster away from you - which is exactly what happened. She ran out to the highway and charged across the traffic without any regard for her own safety, miraculously dodging incoming traffic and making it into a field. I was fairly far behind her, terrified at the notion that this dog was going to take off and not come back - and in the distance covered, I kept jogging after her. After I made it across the highway myself, I got within twenty feet before she sprinted away from me again, speeding across the traffic and onto the property that adjoined my parents' neighborhood. I stayed in pursuit - still barefoot, still on her tail - but as she ran onto the property, I lost her in the trees. As I was looking, a couple drove up with their car and asked if I needed help, and I answered that it would be great - so they let me hop in their car and we drove onto the property to see if we could find her. Spotting her between the trees, I had them pull over and I went after her again, chasing the white flash of fur in the downpour until - again, she ran across teh highway. At this point, I'd been running for about a mile, all barefoot. I took off across the highway one more time, seeing her running across an open field toward the Potomac river. I kept after her, footfalls steady, until I saw her stopped at a tributary along the river, afraid to get into the water, which presented me with the perfect opportunity to pounce on her and hold her to the ground. With this, I picked her up and threw her across my back, legs over my shoulders, and I began to walk home. The rain was pouring down on me, and while she struggled on my back, I held her in place, walking back across the fields she'd covered ground on and back to the highway, where I walked alongside the rumble strips, still barefoot, toward the intersection that entered my parents' neighborhood. I got back to a still-empty house, put the dog down on the floor and carefully closed both the garage and the inner door, opting to jump into the shower before my family got back. What I'll always remember about this story is that it was one of the most human moments in my life - one of the times where I felt the most determined to make sure that I could succeed in what I was going after. I chased her down with pure determination, knowing that I wasn't going to give up on the task at hand, and knowing that I could succeed if I set my mind to it.
