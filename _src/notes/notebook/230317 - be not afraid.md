"When the day begins, you will be starting over."

I remember hearing her say this to me while we were both laying on the edge of the bed. It must have been about 4 in the morning, but it was June, so the first streaks of blue light were tinting the sky, the moon long since at bed. I had my arm around her, our bodies reversed, the silhouettes of my houseplants in the corner.

"What do you mean?"
"You're not going to remember today."

I pulled my arm back from her and sat up on my elbow.

"What are you saying?"
"I mean that if you go to sleep, you're going to forget all of today."
"What are you saying?!"

Her eyes were still not looking in my direction, she was still staring out the window, head leaning slightly off the edge of the bed, a sea of hair cascading down toward the floor. I couldn't see a reflection off her irises. Maybe she was sleep talking?

"What are you saying."
"I'm saying that since you woke up yesterday morning, you have been accruing memories from the day. You woke up, you did... I don't know, your morning routine or whatever, went to work, had your day. You met up with me for drinks. We talked, you leaned over and asked me if you could kiss me and we did, and then you invited me here. We had sex, and it was such good sex - I'll remember it for a long time, I promise. But when you go to sleep and wake up tomorrow, you're not going to remember any of it."

She was joking with me. It was hard to tell in the twilight, but she was. She had to be.
"Right, I'm not going to remember any of this, because at some point tonight I gave you the impression that I'm that person, that I'm going to forget you tomorrow, that I'm the sort of person who just moves onto the next person," I laughed, "Well - I'm not. I... I'm not that person. I'm really sorry that you got that impression. I really had a fantastic time. I'd want to see you again." I turned and leaned down, onto my stomach, to get closer to her.
"I really would like keep doing this. You're... special. I'd not have wanted to bring you here if you hadn't been."
Ok - that last part wasn't completely true, but she didn't react. She kept staring out the window, hands weaving on her stomach. I couldn't tell, entirely, I think she was smiling.
"No - I know that. I'm aware - I had a really good time, too. Don't get me wrong. But you're just not going to remember today."

The way she said it sent pins down my back. There was no emotion in it. I tried to remember if I'd ever left my drink with her, if the Negroni I'd ordered tasted funny. I'd had that same Negroni for so long I didn't think to check for that taste. I don't think I had, we'd sat on the edge of the bar, leaning in - I'd asked her if I could put my hand on her lap when we had gotten closer to speak over the music. She'd been smiling, it was so genuine I... There's no way. I never left my drink.

"I didn't drug you." She chuckled when she said it. I could hear the smile on her voice. It was warm. She pulled herself back onto the bed and looked at me - now the light was enough that I could see her. She didn't look any different, it was that same dark skin I'd been so close to the whole night, the same lips, the same eyes I could barely see, now reflecting what little light was in here, the static from the bed lifting her curled hair up here and there into a halo. She propped herself up onto her elbows and looked at me squarely.

"I didn't do this to you, I promise. This isn't about anything you did, this isn't about anything that is intended upon you. No one chose that this was going to happen. No one has control over it - but you're not going to remember it."

She put her weight on one elbow and reached an arm out - tentative, asking for permission, and when I felt the skin of her fingers on my cheek, I leaned into it, her palm cupping the outside of my cheek. Why did I feel so safe?

"I don't want you to be alarmed. You can try to get up and stay awake, you can attempt to stay awake as long as you'd like - I wouldn't recommend it, it won't make a difference, and there's frankly not a reason to. I'm so sorry that you have to know this."
"I - I - don't know what I'm supposed to do right now. What did you do to me?"
She smiled again across the dark. "I didn't do anything. I'm going to get up and leave soon, and you can choose to do what you like with what I've told you, but as I said, it's not going to make a difference. I wanted to tell you because it's not often that I so enjoy time with someone, it's not often that I get to move through this experience and feel the joy of connecting with someone, and I truly liked you. I liked the story you spun, the half-truths you offered me space to fill, the full truths you cast toward vulnerability. I know all of it was real, and I'm in this room with you because I wanted to be - you never made me feel otherwise. It made me feel safe. I never get to feel safe, I always get to feel alone,"

"So this was good. It is something I wanted to keep, and I wanted to tell you this because I don't often get a chance to tell someone this. I don't often feel a connection with someone, a chance to feel close and understand what it could be like to be close. You were so tender. All of this, the whole night. I loved it. I wanted to tell at least one person that I cared about this, that I want you to remember this, because I'm the only person who will, but that I know you won't."

She sat up in the bed, then moved to the edge of it.
"I never get to share in this moment, what it is that you get to experience, the closeness that you get to feel that is so fleeting, I never get to share in what it means to keep going."
She stood up, straightened out, stretched. Her shoulder blades were silent crescents in blue light.
"But don't want you to feel like this is something bad. You don't have a choice as to whether or not it's happening. You do have a choice on how you respond to it."

She turned toward me again. It felt what little light was in the room wasn't coming from the window. "Be not afraid."
