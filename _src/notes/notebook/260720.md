### In response to [[Mona Alsubei]]'s most recent Substack post
> At that point, I was giving up the lighting industry. I knew it. It moved too fast. I didn’t know how long I’d be gone. But I knew the technology would change. The people would change. Everything would change.
[...]
**Wonder dissolves age. Nobody is old in front of something they’ve never seen before.**

Career changes are strange like this - the realization that you may have niched down onto the frontier of whatever vehicle is encouraging forward change, then realizing that it requires an enormous amount of emotional and constitutional energy to stay there.
It's no surprise that in the modern age we are seeing such an increase in burnout or sabbatical remove in the workforce; we have developed so many new frontiers that anyone who stays at the front of them often runs out of the energy to keep going. The moment that you stop moving with that momentum, it can feel as though you are being left behind.

What I truly love about this interview is the fact that you've tapped into the sense of fulfillment she has found in not keeping pace, but rather seeking out how the threads of a previous life might fit into whatever personal frontiers can be made instead, making, I think, for a far more fulfilling life.

Gibran once wrote, to the great discredit of those who called him a pop philosopher:
> You have been told also that life is darkness, and in your weariness you echo what was said by the weary.
     And I say that life is indeed darkness save when there is urge,
     And all urge is blind save when there is knowledge,
     And all knowledge is vain save when there is work,
     And all work is empty save when there is love;
     And when you work with love you bind yourself to yourself, and to one another, and to God.

There is a pain in knowing oneself — above specifically we leave behind both the judgement of others and their fixed awareness wholly alone in the experience we are having, carrying something that no one or so few can understand.

Sometimes you just want to do it.
I guess the question then is what I want to do, and I have spent months puzzling over this stupid question. I want not to agonize over the injustice of not having the world hand me my next step.
I think I _do_ need a reason, but for the large part this reason needs to be something to which I am first internally aligned with. I think that's nature, somehow, but as well it's being outside, it's moving one's body, it's caring about things well beyond that which our society desires of us. That is to say, I want people to care about the world with me.
The impression I have is that we're better off chasing the ideas that make us excited about life than those we think we are meant to do because it's the sensible choice. I liked running the goalkeepers today because it made me feel like I am capable, and I'm not especially certain that many people can do what I can.
But I don't want to be doing that without a reason — I'm not about to throw everything behind merely just because I'm good at it. I don't want to end up taking it as a result.
The question is now of what I want on the balance — is it the pursuit of a wild step toward wilderness first, then a "career" second. Norway does seem in a way most like the place that would meet those desires, but I don't think I would be happy there.

On the face of it, what I want to do in the long term is navigate the complex politics of how to make a sustainable future more economically attractive than not doing so. I think we so far only get one earth and the massive challenge of figuring out how to oversee its limitation means that we need to be ready to navigate the work together. I do not yet know what it takes to truly unify humanity.

==Move backward==
We mainly use the internet to produce, consume, and interact with information.

The amount of information reaching people each day is notoriously difficult to measure. One researcher tried to measure this in 2012 at around 34gb per person per day, and increasing by 5% each year, but gigabytes aren't a great unit to measure information in. It's easier to measure it in decisions - and today we're constantly forced to make a choice as to whether or not we actually want to pay attention to something.

Five hundred years ago, a person's world was small enough that almost everything they encountered came from a few dozen known people and places in a day — nothing like the volume, or the novelty, we're asked to process now. It's too much to fully interact with, but that's what we're dealing with nowadays. It's no wonder we feel underwater.

With so much of human interaction brought into the information age and channeled the attention economy, this is even more difficult. How do we respond to such an increase in bids for attention?

In most contexts, we do it by reducing the complexity of our interactions - instead of actually sitting with our reaction and coming up with a good response to how it makes us feel, we respond with the simplest tool that we have on hand: the like, the heart, the thumbs up, or the emoji react, and sometimes the gif response.

The like, the heart, the emoji react isn't necessarily meaningless, but it's the only response we feel like is fast enough to survive at that volume. It's not as meaningFUL as saying something more.
==Move toward camera==
So, is a higher volume of overly simplified interaction actually better than less? What comes of those interactions? Does a like actually mean what you want it to mean? Are you developing a relationship between yourself and what you are consuming, and what are the results of those interactions?

When we flatten our interactions to a simple set of reactions that are offered to us, we don't actually get to express how something makes us feel - beyond 'liking' something, we don't ask ourselves what else comes to mind, so all the other emotions vanish under that simple response.

So having watched this video, I'll ask you the question: Having watched this video, what do you feel compelled to do? Do you 'like' this video and move on, or is your emotion more complicated than this?

Let me know, because that's what I'm actually interested in.
