I slept incredibly well last night. I didn't do the work of remembering what I dreamt (I tried to), but I made it into the morning early enough to say that I love waking up early. The first few hours of the day will always feel freer than the others: they are normally outside the territory of any authority, and they feel unusually liberated from the demands of productivity and capitalism.
It's maybe for this reason that these are the hours I treasure most for creativity. My mind is free to wander and be "unproductive," glancing off one thought or another on its course toward or through the reading I've done for the morning. I often feel much more license to explore my thoughts, because there is no one demanding that I put them in service of their work.

When I think about what this means, I generally land on the assessment that I have a little demon in my head threatening me with a hammer any time I start thinking about relaxing. The little demon threatens me with the knowledge that any free time not utilized as a vehicle for productive capital is somehow wasted time. I find that even on the days when I have a very legitimate excuse for not being productive (vacations, illness, days when I "just can't"), the little demon with the hammer is even worse, hopping from foot to foot with an impish smile on their face, knowing that I should not by any means expect a moment of rest.

Yesterday was one of those days. The night before, I'd slept horribly due to the unexpected consumption of a caffeinated tea at 9 p.m., and I was up until 3:30 in the morning reading as a result. I finished two books in direct succession. It was delightful! And I paid for it all day yesterday, as the little purple imp in my brain demanded I produce something of value in response to the productive late-night hours of reading. One of the books I read was *Perfection* by Vincenzo Latronico. It sent me into a spiral, and that spiral is what forced me to finish Lindsay Hill's *Sea of Hooks*, which has a cathartic-while-tragic ending that made its strange micro-chapter structure utterly worthwhile.

Naturally, when I woke up yesterday, I was incredibly motivated to write about either. What does it even mean to read a book if you are not somehow turning your enjoyment into some material insight that you can then place in front of others as an indication of personal legitimacy?

However, my need for proper rest had other plans. I am 36, practically middle-aged, which means that not getting my structured 7–9 hours of sleep leaves me an absolute pile of grey [slurry?] the next day, and nothing meaningful will happen with the day. Part of getting older has meant accepting this fact and creating room for it, which meant that, as a formerly over-employed ex-tech worker without any meaningful job prospects or vision for my future against the incoming job apocalypse, I could "allow myself to relax" and "do less with my day."

Which absolutely did not work. Being utterly exhausted, I resigned myself to thinking that maybe I could simply pick up another book to read, but since my well-rested self tends to over-aspire as to the literature I intend to consume, my pile of to-be-read books is filled with only challenging, vaguely [illegible: two words] books. My brain couldn't do it. [So be it?]

I had some accounting to do, and that didn't work either. The moment I sat down to pile my receipts on my desk, my motivation evaporated and I ended up in the kitchen, peering across the shelves of the fridge. (Come to think of it, this is what happens every time I try to do any accounting. I struggle, we struggle.)

At this point I resigned myself to free-writing in my notebook to see if I could tease some sort of insight out of my restlessness, thinking that maybe somewhere in this [illegible] would be a set of reflections on Latronico's *Perfection*. They never came, and besides, what Rosie Spinks had to say on the book is more insightful than anything I'd have had to say anyway (you can and should read it [here](https://rojospinks.substack.com/p/does-where-you-live-matter); in fact, read all of her articles, she's [brilliant](https://rojospinks.substack.com/)). When the Brilliant Thoughts failed to arrive, I felt it: the panic of not knowing why you can't be productive. Mind you, this came after the rationalization that I could "relax" that day, and I had attempted to fight the notion anyway with a string of tasks.

That panic is infuriating to look back on. It doesn't demonstrate some virtue; rather, it indicates that part of the death drive our society instills in us is the inability to take a fucking break. I'll admit openly that upon realizing I had no cognitive capacity available for the day, I turned even to social media, and the work I have done to wrest myself free of consumption addiction proved itself useful here: Instagram, even, was deeply unsatisfying. Eventually I gave up and stared at the wall instead, my emotions [still?] racing through my chest, and by the time I'd actually checked in with my body, I realized I was incredibly, deeply tired. I fell asleep on the couch within ten minutes.

And you know what? It helped. I didn't wake up and suddenly have the energy to write a panegyric essay on Hill's *Sea of Hooks*, but I felt calmer. I had more clarity. I was better able to accept that the day was not going to mean anything, and I went out on a walk with my dog instead.
