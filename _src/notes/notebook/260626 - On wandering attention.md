Constantly I am urged to solve my wandering attention with some panacea — all I need is a system. All I need is a one-shot solution to solve it all.
I'll admit — the prospect is enticing. My inclination is and has been a burden my entire adult life, and I am constantly fighting the foraging action that pulls me off task, only to find myself suddenly (I swear to god this has happened) suddenly playing a violin in my bedroom, the intent to get on with it utterly and blissfully forgotten.
As a result, one-shot solutions like keeping a stack of lists, or putting up sticky notes — or whatever else is offered in my direction — are all enticing. I have not staying on task. The number of times in my life where I have been reduced to the indirect realization that I have, once again, been forced to pay the "ADHD tax" is a number so large I wish it was my bank account balance itself. What is worse is the certainty that I am not alone in this journey, and that my experience with four has been long-established enough to recognize that in the flurry of "systems" I have sought to implement in order to change one of my afflictions, there really isn't a convenient solution. Everything about trying to remember and to struggle through the toil of keeping on is difficult. I say this knowing intimately that I have failed myself so many times as to wince at the thought.
Despite this, I have seen success in my life: I was a successful photographer for around 8 years. I have done more than most people might think of doing in several lifetimes, and for a brief period of time, I was a tech CEO. All "impressive" things by most measures, but I am here to say, in explicit detail, I struggled through all of it. It is easy for this to be called "high-functioning ADHD" — or whatever name we want to offer it — but the truth is that I really did struggle. Half the time that I have been "succeeding" at anything, I have been white-knuckling my way through the work in pursuit of its completion. There is a reason that this has worked for me, but it is not a one shot solution.
The reason that I've been able to do any of this is that I am stubborn. That's it. It is the reason I have been able to develop discipline and have plunged into the hard work of anything: to do it all not in spite of myself.
If we take a step back, we can get a slightly better picture of what exactly this means. I often see (very inspiring) videos about people's journeys toward success, and usually among the barriers to success are framed as being in the hands of other people: they always mention, never identify first "haters." What baffles me just a bit when I see these videos is that I'm often caught wondering who exactly these "haters" are, — who has the level of anti-capital within them so pronounced as to rise in the face of those who struggle toward success and to tell them that they won't.
My greatest error is that I don't think "haters" actually exist — or rather they themselves, I do think wondering who these "haters" are confuses me greatly because I know my greatest hater: me. I inevitably believe that I can't do something. It could be that this is some survival function in my brain trying to protect me from harm, but being that the things this part of my brain says to me are so harsh as to outpace anything anyone could ever dream up, it's difficult to manage worse haters. This is why I have I am a struggle: no amount of barriers put up by someone else are worse or more powerful than those I impose upon myself.
It would be really easy after this to devolve into a hackneyed, grit-and-determination parable on struggle and particularly struggles with our own afflictions, set of values. That isn't what I am trying to do here. I'm not even sure I am offering advice, rather thinking my way around the reality of my experience to explore why necessity is a heat that I keep stoking up. It is just perspective, which is all we really have in this life. The reason I have been able to keep going despite the difficulty and the mosquito-like whine in my ear to stop is because I am stubborn — I show up entirely in spite of myself.
It's a bit of a reaction often to the fact that I have my hard days where I am exhausted and find it incredibly hard to get up, and then am peppered by a world that truly does think that each person's system or perspective somehow can resolve for another.
