I got three steps into the first chamber before I realized that I'd made a mistake, and that the door closing behind me was something I wasn't comfortable with. From the crinkling sound of the suit, the temperature in the room was lower than I had realized it was, and the plastic immediately stiffened. I knew it was only a matter of time and lack of movement that the temperature would get lower and lower. I wasn't wearing enough clothes and the negative pressure fan was not going to help, pulling in cold, filtered air.

"You ok, Ahrens?"

"Yeah. Yes," I lied. No way out of here but forward.

I walked toward a bright white laminate desk, littered with papers, notebooks, and a microscope. A single dim orange lamp cast a warm glow into the shadows of the surrounding room. In the center of the desk was a small metal container with a ring of dust around it that looked as though it had exploded out of the container, creating a dark gray halo around where it lay, gray fingerprints tracing around the edges of the dust. He was sitting there, grey skinned, immobile, hands on the arms of the his chair, staring into nothing, until his eyes looked up at me again. Red ringed the outside of his irises.

"Hell of a day for this to happen."

His voice sounded like paint dust, his lips barely moving. I kept my distance, hesitant.

"You know, I really didn't think you'd end up being the one coming in here. I'm sorry that it's you."

I nodded inside the negative pressure suit, which he didn't see.

"Yeah. ... yeah."

"Look I don't like this any more than you do, and there ain't nothin we can do right now but try to figure it out. Obviously I don't think I have a ton of time left to think about it with you"

"Right"

"We don't know who sent us this, obviously, but we you and I both know what it is. We have to hope like hell that it didn't make its way out of the room."

"Yeah"

"So I want to know, are they planning for it. Are they worried about it. Did they listen to my instructions"

I relaxed a little bit.

"Yeah. There's an electromagnetic containment device over the whole building. The CDC scrambled a little bit getting a containment bubble that was big enough, but you've only been here for like twelve hours, so ... I guess that's pretty fast."

"Right. We have to hope like hell that was fast enough. You and I both know it probably wasn't."

I nodded. He was probably right. This stupid suit was probably not enough to make it safe, either. I knew that. We well knew that. Again, he didn't see my nod due to the plastic visor.

"Yeah. I'm... yeah. Dr Budoise, I'm so sorry. I really am."

"Ain't nothin. This was probably going to happen, right."

Silence rang out for a second, he looked at me again.

"But we have to do everything we can to prevent it from getting worse. Back in the 50's, even when they were afraid the bombs would drop any day, the saying was always 'You should still go out and plant a tree'. You have to hold on to hope, Ahrens."

"And we don't have much time. You and I both know that. I think I've got about ten minutes in me."

The only parts of him that were moving were his mouth and his eyes, and he'd been speaking with as much care to move as little as possible, but I could see the cracks forming along his jawline.

"What's interesting though, is that I can't really feel anything happening. My wondering is if they're eating away at the least important processes first, turning them into raw materials, and leaving as much possible functional for as long as possible. Like, I'd have thought I'd want to cough, but so far, seems like my lungs are pretty clear. They seem to want to keep that going. Vocal cords though, I ain't too sure."

I didn't really know what to do. We were doing everything we could. There was a probability that in the time it took to seal off the building, some of the dust had floated out of the room. I'd been downstairs in the wet lab, sealed off. I hadn't taken off my negative pressure suit in almost a day.

"But I'll be all right, ain't nothin doing. I hope you'll be all right"

"Yeah."
