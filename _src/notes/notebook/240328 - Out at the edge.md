Out at the edge of the L5 cluster, someone turned on an orbital zero-friction ion drive in just the right place and the Path was seen for the first time - a bright green flash that was hanging in orbit, then dimmed to a six-hundred-kilometer streak leading away from Earth. Science craft parked themselves around green light for a number of months, aiming to gather measurements of whatever it was that had been sparked into being around ignition, but any sensor type was only capable of detecting a 555 nanometer wavelength hanging in the air with no source - simply emerald green light floating in fixed orbit, without a source.
What made the beam more interesting than an immaculately conceived lightbeam sitting in space was that it changed direction with the orbit of earth around the planet, fixed on an unknown location off in the distance, wavering with the change of the L5's place in orbit.

The positioning of the beam relative to the earth's own orbit presented a problem. When the sun and the earth occluded the path, it showed up as a harmless 4-kilometer-wide beam that passed directly though anything in its path, inexplicably illuminating the insides of buildings as it passed through - but anything in the path would be cast in a fixed level of brightness that engulfed everything. Those who lived in its occlusion path would complain that they couldn't escape the light, but there was no getting around it - when the beam passed through the Earth, everything was green.
This lead to a divergence in responses - most populations sitting in the path of occlusion migrated away from this annoyance once the novelty had worn off - others thought that the beam was a sign from their god and that because of this, they should live directly in the path of the light. The science said nothing - it was harmless green light, but an annoyance, and there was nothing do be done about it, and no escaping the light if you were in its path - but green-light research centers as well built themselves into the occlusion path in order to better understand properties of the light.
About two years after the beam flashed into being, observers still paying attention noticed as well that the beam was slowly moving along its fixed trajectory, leading sages in Elysium City to plot the beam's trajectory out of our system, and to it's eventual name as 'The Path'. The movement of the beam was measurably slow - on the order of at less than a meter per year, but work put into measurement proved that this movement was accelerating over time - prompting sages to call for bigger investment from their parishes to study the phenomenon in the hopes that it would be understood before it accelerated out of our system. A number of probes were sent out ahead of its path as well - many-paneled stations built to either prepare for its passing, or in the assumed trajectory of its movement.

After ten years, the Path's movement had accelerated to a rate of two hundred meters per year with no sign of deceleration, and no progress had been made on the meaning of either the trajectory or of the beam itself - the rate of acceleration was too high for any meaningful propulsion mechanism to catch up once it was to reach high speed, and there was no telling if it would accelerate to the some unknown physical constant.
