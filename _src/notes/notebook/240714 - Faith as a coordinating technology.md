[[faith]] is a coordinating technology - it's well suited to the direction of huge groups of people because it is a conclusive and motivated set of traditions and actions that are well-connected to the utility of a common goal

this is the most interesting thing about coordination as rooted in faith, and it is indeed what is missing from the human species.

the act of believing some set of conditions will improve is based on a common ability to believe in the improvement of these conditions as *exclusive* of free will -- that is, *faith is the act of believing that there will be a better outcome despite not knowing for certain that the outcome will happen*.

faith is inimitably tied as well to hope, because hope is just as blind, while absolving itself of the lack of agency that faith inspires in some. if a sociological technology of faith is possible (and it is because we have evolved it in several directions as a species), then it's still possible to use that technology for the better; it's possible to use both faith and hope in the service of good things

this is in some ways what I think we have lost as a civilization: the inability to have faith in a collectively-held outcome that serves as many people as possible.

the effect of lacking an alternative executable collective coordination mechanism that efficiently orients humans in the same direction is that it results in a lack of commonly-desired outcomes: without an effective means through which to point all of us at something that all of us want, we each think that we want something different from our neighbor, either locally or on much larger scales. this results in coordination failure -- we don't move in the same direction, even when it could benefit all of us.

this is the danger of not having a meaningful coordination technology at the level of a species; now there is no way for us to meaningfully work together to solve collective problems.

this is why I think that we need to reconsider the social definition of faith and it's utility in how we approach collective problems.
