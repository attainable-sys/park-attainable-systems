In order to get things done, there are basically two pressures: **Time** and **Money**

**When you do not have time, you have to spend it preciously. When you have too much time, you will waste it.**

- this notion comes from understanding that broadly, it's hard to urge forward a system if it's trying to grow under conditions that threaten its ability to exist. This will result in quick movement in vectors that preserve the system to the best of the ability for it to stabilize and maintain itself.

- If, by contrast, time is abundant, then there will be little effort by the system to do more than expand readily, taking chances in directions that might not actually favor the system at large, resulting in a need for the system to respond by reorienting toward the highest-value paths in times of scarcity.

**When you need to make money, you will work harder toward earning it. When you have money, you will work harder spending it.**

- When resources are scarce but a system is still in need of them, the system will work to do two things: gain its resources as widely as possible, and use the resources at its disposal to move in the direction of the largest possible source of those resources.
- If, on the other hand, resources are plentiful, it's less likely that the system will have to work hard to allocate resources effectively toward the corporeal needs that are the highest priority. This results in the same issue that we see with the abundance of time - the system grows too widely and the misuse of resources results in outcomes that spread resources thin in an attempt to spread across a possible domain as widely as possible.

With these in mind, it's important to note that in times of abundance, chances are taken that may be costly when scarcity approaches. these two forces will happen in concert and will result in opposing degrees of preparedness on the part of the system - _knowing full well that the approach is always unlikely to be perfect and that some degree of failure needs to be tolerated in order for chances to keep the system surviving._

The notion that systems should move perfectly is inorganic - it is not in line with the ways that natural systems function. Recombination in times of abundance means that the cause for more variation can result in resilience, but the meaning of this resilience _is only apparent in times of scarcity_.

This means that when approaching the notion of how to build out a system effectively, it's increasingly important to take chances when there is a lot at your disposal, but it's equally important to be ready to eat the sacrifices that emerge as a result of this when scarcity comes around. It cannot seem like a failure, but rather a lesson, and a meaningful one at that.

This is why in times of crisis, the most important tracks are the ones that have been used and re-used. Access and examine the paths that have been used before and refine them. Scarcity is a cruel, but diligent teacher. Be sure to be one that is listening when it offers a lesson, and dig into what you know in order to push forward.
