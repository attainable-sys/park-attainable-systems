Passion does not require meaning.

At least, not directly. The importance of being able to do great things with the intent to resolve them into action only requires a directed amount of energy and the direction in which it is delivered. Passion then only requires care, the ability to direct this energy with a level of care that comes from a requirement to commit to resolution. Meaning is something altogether different, something that comes from a different source. Connecting the two is important, but they don't need to come from the same thing.

I'd always puzzled over this fact. The reason is that I care way too much, but then I start feeling a deep incongruence between the amount that I care and the amount of meaning that I derived from the work - and when we lose meaning (one of the most profound of the human afflictions) there is no coming back from having set up the wrong logic on why to continue on with what we're attempting to set ourselves in motion to do.

Meaning is altogether a different set of emotions from passion - it encompasses the _reasons_ we do what it is we set ourselves in motion to do. Meaning is crucial to knowing why you are doing something in the long term - meaning is the motivator that keeps a person going when all else seems too difficult to proceed with. It's all too easy to conflate meaning with passion because they do rhyme very well - they are emotional cousins.

However, conflating them too much is a recipe for burning out, especially when a passionate person places too much care into the work they are doing and their energy expenditure outweighs the logic behind the approach. The reason that passion is also an emotion that fits in the realm of love is because passion is meaningless - it lacks definition and is simply an engine that is running in the direction of some corporeal motivation for connection, but meaning is not necessary to this.

The expression to a loved one that they give your life meaning is usually incorrect; a lover does not offer meaning, they offer connection, the meaning is derived from this connection attaching itself to the importance of that connection to your personal goals. Meaning is wholly in the realm of the personal, internal emotions. When we connect it too much to something external, we are offering these external things the power over our ability to survive, and this is not sustainable, in fact it is dangerous to do so. Meaning requires contemplation. Passion does not, in many cases it is automatic.

Passion is an emotion of the present, by contrast - it is something that arrives from a compulsion. It's for this reason that when people have told me that they assume I am passionate about the things I am doing, I have conflated their words with the search for meaning - I am not thinking in the present when I retort that I am not. I am removing the importance of the passion in the moment in which it is being experienced. Other people, in this moment, are right. To have passion is only to have directed energy and care toward something in order to see action take place.

There's an important implication here: passion without meaning in the long term is not sustainable. It is possible to use passion as its own motivator, as something that accelerates our actions into more vigorous attention and completion, but in the long term those actions will need to be paired with some sort of meaning in order to see out their impact on the larger arc of our lives. Meaning, in this case, is the reason we do something, and passion is how we ensure that we can keep doing so.

Passion without having searched for the meaning behind the actions - whether connected or not - becomes a drain. I mentioned above - passion is energy directed with care toward an outcome. Energy requires resources. If we are not being replenished in the actions that we are taking because they are not leading toward something, finding the energy to keep going becomes immensely difficult.

Additionally, meaning found in those things that aren't related to who we are as people and what motivates us more widely to keep going is a careful balance. Meaning is not monolithic, ideally it is a constellation of aggregated sources. It's important that these sources are both inside and outside of the self; those things toward from which we derive meaning must have as much to do with who we are as people as they are important to missions that exist outside of our own area of effect. Meaning is the source from which we can draw the energy to keep going when passion has us in motion.

Meaning is specifically not something that need be _related_ to the actions motivated by passion. It is possible to do something with immense passion because it serves a meaning that exists outside the realm of direct effect; it is possible to work a job passionately because you want to provide for your family. They do not require a direct connection between one another.

This is how we do hard things that don't have meaning. Meaning is found from wherever the action meets what is most important to us. This is why so much work can cause a moral panic - when we place too much importance on the actions taking place as those from which we are meant to derive meaning, we often find that the actions themselves can remain quite empty. Sometimes the work that we are doing isn't necessarily meaningful. Meaning is elsewhere. Meaning is bigger than the passion with which we perform our actions

Finding the capability to hold on to this truth while we do work gives us the motivation to keep going: it is possible to find meaning outside of your work, and to use that meaning to motivate the passion with which you do it. This way, even when the work becomes something that drains you, meaning can be something larger, something more removed. If meaning and passion are connected, that's great - but it's not often the case. I'd even argue that it's dangerous to always try to pair the two. Searching for meaning requires energy as well, and often working with passion requires so much of our energy that if we are simultaneously attempting to fork what little energy we have toward these two intensive actions, it is costly, and specifically costly in ways that lead toward moral and energetic panic.

The reason I share this is because I have fallen into this trap so many times in my life, and it's often what starts to close the door on the reasons I keep going. I need my work to be important to me, but it's equally imperative that I explore that importance. It makes sense to search for meaning in the work that you are doing, but it also matters that you take care of yourself when you do. You can't and shouldn't do things entirely to the benefit of external sources, because your work will never love you back, and most of the energy you have available for love should be spent on the things that will. These are the sources that will give you the most meaning, at the end of a day. Very few things in your life will care about you as much as you will care about yourself.
